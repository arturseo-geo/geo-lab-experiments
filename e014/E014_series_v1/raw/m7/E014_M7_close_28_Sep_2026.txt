How do AI search engines select content to cite?

AI search engines typically use a multi-stage retrieval and ranking process:
- Retrieve relevant pages using embeddings or keyword matching
- Filter and re-rank by factors like topical relevance, clarity, and factual grounding
- Extract passages that support the answer and attach verifiable sources
- Prefer fresh, verifiable, and clearly interpretable content; many retrieved pages may not be cited in the final answer

Citations are chosen based on how well the content supports the question, its trust signals, and its ability to be attributed, not simply on domain authority. [stellar-ai](https://stellar-ai.co/blog/how-ai-search-engines-decide-what-to-cite/)

What is System Memory in GEO?


## Meaning in GEO

In **Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)**, **System Memory** means the persistent association an AI system develops between your brand, website, or author and a particular topic over time.

It is often described as the fifth layer of the GEO Stack: **“Does the AI remember you?”**—not necessarily as literal memory in every AI system, but as accumulated recognition through repeated retrievals, citations, consistent terminology, and reliable content. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

## How it develops

System Memory tends to strengthen when you:

- Use the same brand and entity names consistently.
- Publish regularly within a clear topical area.
- Build multiple related pages that reinforce one another.
- Keep information accurate, current, and structurally consistent.
- Earn repeated citations or mentions across trusted sources.

These patterns help generative systems form a stable understanding of what your site is about and where it is authoritative. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/system-memory/)

## Example

Suppose a company consistently publishes high-quality material about **technical SEO for ecommerce**, uses the same organization name and author information, updates its guides, and is repeatedly cited by AI systems.

Over time, an AI tool may be more likely to associate that company with ecommerce technical SEO and include it when answering related questions. That accumulated association is **System Memory**.

## Important limitation

System Memory is generally a **long-term outcome**, not a single setting you can directly optimise. It emerges from sustained consistency across other GEO factors—such as retrieval, extractability, entity clarity, and structural authority—over months or longer. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

How to measure AI citation rate?

AI citation rate is measured as the percentage of AI-generated answers (across a fixed set of test queries and platforms) that cite or mention your brand, domain, or URL. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

## Core formula

The standard definition used by most practitioners is:

**AI citation rate = (answers citing or mentioning your brand ÷ total test queries) × 100%** [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

Variants exist depending on what you count as a “citation”:

- **Brand/domain mention rate**: any answer that names your brand or domain in the prose. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)
- **Source-citation rate**: any answer that lists your URL in its sources/footnotes. [citability](https://citability.dev/blog/how-to-measure-ai-citation-rate)
- **Qualifying citation rate**: only counts citations that meet predefined rules (e.g., correct context, non-outdated info, specific pages). [babel42](https://babel42.io/blog/ai-citation-rate)

All of these reduce to “cited instances ÷ total valid prompt runs” expressed as a percentage. [prompteden](https://prompteden.com/resources/measuring-ai-citation-rates/)

## Step‑by‑step method to measure it

### 1. Define your query set

Create a fixed list of realistic buyer questions (typically 15–50):

- **Brand queries**: “What does [YourBrand] do?”
- **Market/scenario queries**: “Best tools for [use case]”, “How to solve [problem]”
- **Comparison queries**: “[YourBrand] vs [Competitor]” [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

Market/scenario queries are especially important because they reflect users who don’t already know you. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

### 2. Choose platforms and settings

Test across multiple AI search/experience surfaces, since citation behavior differs:

- ChatGPT (with web/search enabled)
- Perplexity
- Google AI Overviews / Gemini
- Claude (with web/tools if available) [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

For each platform, lock down:

- Model/version (if visible)
- Country/language
- Logged-in vs logged-out
- Web/search on or off
- Date/time of test [siftly](https://siftly.ai/blog/tools-measure-citation-rates-ai-generated-content-brands-2026)

### 3. Run the queries and record results

For each query:

- Paste the exact prompt.
- Save the full answer text.
- Record every source/footnote URL the system shows.
- Mark whether your brand/domain/URL appears in:
  - The answer text (mention)
  - The source list (citation) [citability](https://citability.dev/blog/how-to-measure-ai-citation-rate)

Keep a simple spreadsheet with columns like:

- Query
- Platform
- Date/time
- Brand mentioned? (Y/N)
- Brand cited as source? (Y/N)
- Source URLs
- Notes on accuracy/context [deepsmith](https://deepsmith.ai/blog/measure-ai-citation-rate)

### 4. Define what counts as a citation

Decide your rules before scoring, for example:

- Any page on your domain in sources = citation.
- Only specific product pages count; blog posts don’t.
- Mentions without links count as “mention” but not “citation.” [babel42](https://babel42.io/blog/ai-citation-rate)

Apply these rules consistently to each run.

### 5. Calculate the rate

For a given platform and query set:

\[
\text{Citation rate} = \frac{\text{Number of valid runs with at least one qualifying citation}}{\text{Total valid runs}} \times 100\%
\]  [deepsmith](https://deepsmith.ai/blog/measure-ai-citation-rate)

You can compute:

- **Per-platform rate** (e.g., ChatGPT citation rate, Perplexity citation rate).
- **Overall rate** across all platforms (aggregate numerator and denominator).
- **Domain-level rate** by combining all your URLs. [rankshift](https://www.rankshift.ai/blog/key-ai-search-metrics/)

Example: If you test 20 queries on Perplexity and your domain appears as a source in 3 answers, your Perplexity citation rate is \(3/20 \times 100\% = 15\%\). [citability](https://citability.dev/blog/how-to-measure-ai-citation-rate)

### 6. Track over time

A single measurement is a baseline; trends matter more:

- Re-run the same query set on a fixed cadence (weekly or monthly).
- Keep phrasing and settings as consistent as possible.
- Track changes in citation rate, not just absolute values. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

Use these trends to evaluate the impact of content updates, schema changes, or AI optimization efforts. [amplerank](https://amplerank.ai/how-to/measure-ai-citations)

## Practical tips

- Start small: a 20-query manual test across ChatGPT and Perplexity can give you a usable baseline in under an hour. [citability](https://citability.dev/blog/how-to-measure-ai-citation-rate)
- Don’t rely on one platform; citation patterns differ widely between ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and AI Overviews. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)
- Distinguish between **appearance/mention rate** (brand named in text) and **source citation rate** (URL in footnotes); both are useful but measure different things. [babel42](https://babel42.io/blog/ai-citation-rate)
- For more robust measurement, some teams add:
  - Sentiment or accuracy flags
  - Position of citation (top vs buried in sources)
  - Competitor citations for share-of-voice analysis [deepsmith](https://deepsmith.ai/blog/measure-ai-citation-rate)

If you tell me your industry and typical buyer questions, I can sketch a sample 20-query set and a simple spreadsheet layout tailored to your use case.

Does GEO actually work?

Yes—but **GEO works mainly as a visibility and citation tactic, not as a guaranteed traffic or sales channel**.

## What the evidence shows

The original GEO research found that specific content changes—such as adding relevant quotations, statistics, and source citations—could increase a page’s visibility in generative-engine answers by up to 40% in controlled experiments. The effect varied substantially by topic, method, and engine. [dl.acm](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3637528.3671900)

More recent research is more cautious: it finds evidence that content already retrieved by an AI system can influence the wording and source selection of an answer, but not yet strong evidence of a stable, cross-platform improvement in organic discovery, clicks, or conversions. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14035v1)

So the practical answer is:

| Goal | Does GEO work? |
|---|---|
| Make content easier for AI to understand | Yes |
| Increase the chance of being cited once retrieved | Often |
| Guarantee inclusion in ChatGPT, Perplexity, or AI Overviews | No |
| Consistently increase website traffic | Unproven |
| Replace conventional SEO | No |

## What “GEO” can realistically improve

GEO is most useful when it improves the underlying content:

- Answering the target question directly near the beginning.
- Using clear headings and well-defined entities.
- Including original data, specific examples, and attributable quotations.
- Supporting important claims with credible sources.
- Keeping facts current and making pages easy to crawl and quote.
- Building genuine authority through reputable third-party mentions.

These changes give an AI system clearer, more verifiable material to retrieve and cite. They also tend to be ordinary good SEO and editorial practice—not secret optimizations. Google’s stated position is that optimizing for generative search is still fundamentally SEO, rather than a separate technical system requiring special markup or an `llms.txt` file. [searchenginejournal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-new-ai-search-guide-calls-aeo-and-geo-still-seo/575026/)

## What is probably overhyped

Be skeptical of claims such as:

- “This guarantees ChatGPT citations.”
- “A 40% GEO increase applies to every website.”
- “AI engines reward a particular word count or formatting formula.”
- “GEO alone will produce measurable revenue.”
- “You can manipulate all AI platforms with the same checklist.”

The 40% figure is a **maximum experimental visibility result**, not a typical guaranteed lift. A 2026 review specifically warns that it should not be treated as a general claim and says the strongest evidence concerns influence over already-retrieved content—not downstream clicks or conversions. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14035v1)

## Best way to use it

Treat GEO as an extension of SEO and content quality:

1. Pick questions your audience actually asks.
2. Publish a genuinely authoritative answer.
3. Make key claims precise, extractable, and well sourced.
4. Add original information rather than rephrasing competitors.
5. Track AI citations and qualified traffic separately.
6. Run controlled before-and-after tests instead of trusting visibility scores.

**Bottom line:** GEO can increase the probability that an AI answer understands, mentions, or cites your content. It cannot reliably force inclusion, and evidence that it consistently increases traffic or business outcomes is still limited.

What is Extractability in GEO?

**Extractability** in GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is how easily an AI system can identify, isolate, and accurately reuse a self-contained piece of your content in its answer. In short: if an AI retrieves your page, can it lift a clean, correct “quote-ready” passage without losing meaning? [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)

## Why it matters

A page can be crawlable and relevant but still not cited if its main points are buried in long, dependent paragraphs. Extractability is the structural step between being retrieved and being cited: retrieval gets your content into the AI’s candidate set, while extractability determines whether the AI can actually use it. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-glossary-2026/)

## What makes content extractable

- **Answer-first structure:** State the direct answer in the first sentence of the section. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)
- **Section independence:** Make each heading/section understandable without requiring the preceding paragraphs. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)
- **Explicit entity anchoring:** Name the subject clearly (“Acme’s refund policy”) rather than relying on pronouns or vague references. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)
- **Compression resistance:** Keep the core claim intact even if the passage is reduced to one or two sentences. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)
- **Clear formatting:** Use descriptive headings, short paragraphs, lists, tables, and structured data where appropriate. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/10-extractability-signals-ai-citation/)

## Simple example

Low extractability:

> It depends on several factors, but customers generally have options available, as explained below, though exceptions may apply.

High extractability:

> **What is Acme’s standard refund window?** Acme offers a 30-day refund window from the delivery date for unused items in original packaging.

The second version gives AI systems a compact, unambiguous fact they can lift and cite. [menra](https://www.menra.ai/glossary/extractability)

## Extractability vs. citation-worthiness

Extractability asks whether an AI *can* cleanly quote a passage; citation-worthiness asks whether it *should*—based on accuracy, authority, freshness, and trust. Strong extractability is necessary for reliable AI citation, but it does not replace credibility and content quality. [menra](https://www.menra.ai/glossary/extractability)

How does the GEO Stack work?

The GEO Stack is a layered framework used in Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) to systematically improve how often and how accurately AI systems (like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, etc.) retrieve, understand, and cite your content. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

## Core idea

Rather than treating “AI visibility” as one thing, the GEO Stack breaks it into sequential layers, each representing a distinct failure point in the AI retrieval-and-citation pipeline.  If content fails at a lower layer, higher-layer optimizations can’t help, so the stack is designed to be fixed bottom‑up. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

Different practitioners use slightly different naming, but the most widely referenced version (from The GEO Lab) defines five main layers plus an infrastructure gate: [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/what-is-generative-engine-optimisation/)

- **Layer 0 – Infrastructure Accessibility**  
  Technical gate: can AI crawlers even reach and index your content? This includes `robots.txt`, bot allowances (e.g., GPTBot, PerplexityBot), site uptime, and basic crawlability. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

- **Layer 1 – Retrieval Probability**  
  Determines whether AI systems are likely to retrieve your page when processing a query. Signals include indexing status, internal linking, sitemap coverage, and how well your content matches likely query patterns. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

- **Layer 2 – Extractability**  
  Focuses on whether an AI can cleanly pull a useful answer from your page. Tactics include:  
  - Clear headings and question-style subheads  
  - Direct answers in the first sentence under each heading  
  - Use of lists, tables, FAQs, and well-structured sections  
  - Schema markup (FAQPage, Article, Service, etc.) to make meaning machine-readable [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

- **Layer 3 – Entity Reinforcement**  
  Builds a strong, consistent “entity” signal so AI systems recognize your brand, product, or person as a distinct, trustworthy entity. This covers:  
  - Consistent name, NAP (name/address/phone), and social handles  
  - Schema `sameAs` links to Wikipedia, Wikidata, Crunchbase, etc.  
  - Author schema and bylines  
  - Presence in knowledge bases and high-authority profiles [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

- **Layer 4 – Structural Authority**  
  The trust layer: why should the AI prefer your content over others? Signals include:  
  - Quality backlinks and citations from reputable sites  
  - E‑E‑A‑T signals (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness)  
  - Citation patterns (how often and where you’re referenced) [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

- **Layer 5 – System Memory** (sometimes folded into Layer 4 in simpler models)  
  Keeps your content “alive” in AI systems over time via freshness signals:  
  - Regular updates and new content  
  - Versioned or time-stamped resources  
  - Ongoing mentions and citations that reinforce recency [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

Other variants reframe these layers in more operational terms (e.g., “Business Intelligence & Entity Engineering”, “LLM Training & Entity Injection”, “Citation Engineering”), but they map to the same underlying pipeline: make content accessible → retrievable → extractable → entity-recognized → trusted → persistently cited. [seosmart.co](https://www.seosmart.co.ke/how-to-get-recommended-by-chatgpt-gemini-perplexity/)

## How it works in practice

In practice, you:

1. **Audit from the bottom up**: Check crawler access and indexing first; only then optimize structure, then entity signals, then authority and freshness. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)
2. **Optimize at the section level**: Not just whole pages, but individual chunks that AI might quote (e.g., a specific FAQ or definition block). [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)
3. **Measure AI citation rate**: Track how often your brand/content appears in AI-generated answers for target queries, then iterate on the weakest layer. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

GEO vs traditional SEO — what changed?

The biggest change is the unit of value: traditional SEO optimizes for **rankings and clicks** to your site, while GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) optimizes for **inclusion and citations inside AI-generated answers** that may never send a click. [wboc](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/tech_talk_and_innovation/what-todays-pr-and-marcom-pros-need-to-know-about-seo-and-geo/article_1a39d112-d4fd-55cf-96ac-b9bdc52466e9.html)

## What GEO is (and isn’t)

GEO is the practice of making your content easy for large language models (LLMs) and AI search experiences (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews/AI Mode, Gemini, Claude) to **understand, trust, and quote** when synthesizing answers.  It’s not a replacement for SEO; it’s an extension aimed at a different output format: summarized, conversational responses instead of a list of blue links. [susodigital](https://susodigital.com/thoughts/seo-vs-geo/)

## What actually changed

### 1) Goal and success metric

- **Traditional SEO:** Win top-10 rankings → earn clicks → convert on-site. Success = rankings, CTR, organic sessions, conversions. [strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo-guide)
- **GEO:** Win inclusion in AI answers → earn citations/mentions → influence decisions before the click. Success = citation rate, “in-answer” presence, brand association with a category. [wboc](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/tech_talk_and_innovation/what-todays-pr-and-marcom-pros-need-to-know-about-seo-and-geo/article_1a39d112-d4fd-55cf-96ac-b9bdc52466e9.html)

### 2) How content is consumed

- **SEO:** Humans scan a SERP, choose a result, then read your full page. [wboc](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/tech_talk_and_innovation/what-todays-pr-and-marcom-pros-need-to-know-about-seo-and-geo/article_1a39d112-d4fd-55cf-96ac-b9bdc52466e9.html)
- **GEO:** AI reads many sources, decides what’s relevant/true, and writes its own answer; your page may be used as a source without the user ever visiting it. [wboc](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/tech_talk_and_innovation/what-todays-pr-and-marcom-pros-need-to-know-about-seo-and-geo/article_1a39d112-d4fd-55cf-96ac-b9bdc52466e9.html)

That shifts the emphasis from “get the click” to “be the source the model trusts and cites.” [wboc](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/tech_talk_and_innovation/what-todays-pr-and-marcom-pros-need-to-know-about-seo-and-geo/article_1a39d112-d4fd-55cf-96ac-b9bdc52466e9.html)

### 3) Ranking signals vs. citation signals

Core authority still matters in both, but the weighting changes:

- **SEO signals:** Backlinks, anchor text, domain authority, on-page keyword usage, technical SEO (Core Web Vitals, sitemaps, etc.). [strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo-guide)
- **GEO signals:** Clear entities, factual consistency, unique data points, corroboration across multiple sources, explicit provenance/metadata, machine-readable structure. [strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo-guide)

LLMs care less about classic “keyword density” and more about **entity clarity and cross-source agreement**. [strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo-guide)

### 4) Content structure and format

- **SEO-friendly pages:** Long-form, human-oriented articles with hero images, meta descriptions, internal links, and keyword-rich headings. [strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo-guide)
- **GEO-friendly content:** Concise, definitive answers early (often in the first ~50 words), clear sections, bullet points, consistent terminology, and structured data so models can extract and attribute claims. [strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo-guide)

In practice, you still write for humans, but you make key facts **easy to extract and attribute**. [thriveagency](https://thriveagency.com/news/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo/)

### 5) Technical layer

- **SEO:** Crawlability, indexability, site speed, mobile-friendliness, hreflang, XML sitemaps. [strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo-guide)
- **GEO:** All of the above, plus stronger emphasis on schema, explicit entity linking, provenance metadata, and sometimes JSON/API endpoints so AI systems can parse and verify your data. [strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo-guide)

### 6) Measurement and attribution

- **SEO dashboards:** Impressions, clicks, average position, landing-page performance. [susodigital](https://susodigital.com/thoughts/seo-vs-geo/)
- **GEO dashboards:** Visibility in AI Overviews/AI Mode, frequency of citations/mentions by AI assistants, brand association in answers, assisted conversions where the AI answer influenced the journey. [singlegrain](https://www.singlegrain.com/aeo/geo-vs-seo-vs-aeo-the-future-of-search-optimization/)

Attribution gets harder because the “last click” may never happen; the decisive moment is often the AI summary. [susodigital](https://susodigital.com/thoughts/seo-vs-geo/)

## What *hasn’t* changed much

Many experts argue GEO/AEO are **SEO fundamentals applied to a new output**, not a wholly new discipline:

- Authority, relevance, accuracy, and trust remain central. [bignewsnetwork](https://www.bignewsnetwork.com/news/279319518/aeo-and-geo-explained-why-experts-say-it-still-just-dental-seo-with-a-few-changes)
- High-quality, well-structured, original content still wins. [windmillstrategy](https://www.windmillstrategy.com/seo-vs-geo/)
- Technical hygiene (fast, crawlable, secure sites) is still required. [thriveagency](https://thriveagency.com/news/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo/)

The core playbook is similar; the **definition of visibility** and the **metrics you optimize for** have shifted. [susodigital](https://susodigital.com/thoughts/seo-vs-geo/)

## Practical takeaway

If you already do solid SEO, you’re partly “GEO-ready.” To tilt toward GEO:

- Put clear, factual answers and definitions near the top of key pages. [strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo-guide)
- Use consistent entity names and structured data (schema) to reduce ambiguity. [strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo-guide)
- Publish unique data, case studies, and verifiable claims that models can cite. [strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo-guide)
- Track not just rankings and traffic, but also presence in AI Overviews and AI assistants. [singlegrain](https://www.singlegrain.com/aeo/geo-vs-seo-vs-aeo-the-future-of-search-optimization/)

In short: SEO gets you ranked; GEO gets you **quoted**. [wboc](https://www.wboc.com/online_features/tech_talk_and_innovation/what-todays-pr-and-marcom-pros-need-to-know-about-seo-and-geo/article_1a39d112-d4fd-55cf-96ac-b9bdc52466e9.html)

What is LLM readability?

**LLM readability** refers to how easily a large language model (such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google’s AI Overviews) can parse, segment, understand, and extract reliable information from a piece of content—especially at the level of individual passages or “chunks.” [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)

It is distinct from *human* readability, which measures how easy text is for people to read and comprehend. A page might flow well for humans but perform poorly for LLMs if its sections depend heavily on surrounding context, lack clear structure, or use ambiguous language. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)

## Why LLM readability matters

Modern AI search and answer engines don’t ingest whole pages as continuous prose. Instead, they:

- Break content into smaller chunks (often 40–80 words or a single clear idea per paragraph). [ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/how-to-make-content-llm-readable/)
- Embed each chunk into a vector representation. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)
- Retrieve and cite the chunks that best match a user’s query. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)

If your content has high LLM readability, it’s more likely to be:

- Correctly understood by the model. [ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/how-to-make-content-llm-readable/)
- Retrieved in response to relevant queries. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)
- Quoted or cited in AI-generated answers. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)

This is a core goal of **LLM Optimization (LLMO)**: shaping content so AI systems can reliably extract and trust it. [ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/how-to-make-content-llm-readable/)

## Key dimensions of LLM readability

Sources describe several overlapping factors that determine how “LLM-readable” content is: [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)

- **Chunking / passage independence**  
  Each section should make sense on its own, without needing earlier or later paragraphs to be understood. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)

- **Clear heading structure (H1–H3)**  
  Logical, descriptive headings act like a table of contents for AI, signaling what question or topic each section addresses. [ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/how-to-make-content-llm-readable/)

- **Answer-first writing**  
  Starting sections with a direct, factual statement (then adding nuance) improves the chance of verbatim citation. [ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/how-to-make-content-llm-readable/)

- **Natural language quality & lexical clarity**  
  Clear definitions, consistent terminology, and minimal unexplained jargon help models assign confident meanings to your text. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)

- **Information hierarchy & context management**  
  Organizing from general to specific, and avoiding heavy reliance on implicit context, makes embeddings more accurate. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)

- **Formatting that aids extraction**  
  Short paragraphs, bullet lists, numbered steps, and tables are easier for LLMs to parse than dense blocks of prose or text embedded in images. [ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/how-to-make-content-llm-readable/)

- **Entity consistency**  
  Using the same names for brands, products, people, and concepts throughout avoids confusing the model’s internal “meaning graph.” [ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/how-to-make-content-llm-readable/)

## How it’s measured (in practice)

While there’s no single universal score, tools and frameworks often look at signals like: [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)

- Percentage of question-based headings. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)
- Average paragraph length (often targeting under ~400 characters). [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)
- Consistency of core terms (n-gram density). [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)
- Presence of explicit definitions and summary paragraphs near the top. [ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/how-to-make-content-llm-readable/)
- Structural clarity (proper use of H1/H2/H3, lists, tables). [ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/how-to-make-content-llm-readable/)

Some SEO tools now offer an “LLM readability score” that combines these factors into a composite metric indicating how likely an AI system is to extract and cite your page. [sorank](https://www.sorank.com/seo-tool/llm-readability-checker)

## LLM readability vs. traditional readability metrics

Traditional readability formulas (e.g., Flesch–Kincaid, grade-level scores) estimate how hard text is for *humans* based on sentence length, word length, syllables, etc. [ai.jmir](https://ai.jmir.org/2026/1/e77149)

LLM readability, by contrast, is about:

- How well isolated chunks produce coherent embeddings. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)
- How confidently an AI can extract a factual answer from that chunk. [ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/how-to-make-content-llm-readable/)
- How likely the chunk is to be selected and cited in AI-generated responses. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-the-most-influential-factors-to-become-citation-worthy-by-llms)

A text can have a good human readability score but still be hard for LLMs to use if it’s poorly structured, context-dependent, or semantically ambiguous. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)

What is Retrieval Probability in AI search?

Retrieval Probability is the estimated likelihood that a given content unit will be surfaced by an AI retrieval system (e.g., a search or RAG pipeline) and thus appear in the initial candidate results. It models how likely the content is to be retrieved before any ranking or reranking steps. [nebulatech]

What is GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation)?

**Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)** is the practice of structuring digital content and managing your online presence so that generative AI systems (like Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Gemini) cite, summarise, or recommend your brand when answering user queries. [searchengineland](https://searchengineland.com/what-is-generative-engine-optimization-geo-444418)

## How GEO differs from traditional SEO

| Traditional SEO | Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) |
|-----------------|--------------------------------------|
| Targets ranking in a list of blue links (SERPs) | Targets inclusion inside AI-generated answers |
| Optimises for keywords and backlinks | Optimises for clarity, authority, structure, and citability |
| Success = clicks to your site | Success = being named, quoted, or linked in an AI response |
| Focuses on your website | Focuses on your whole indexed ecosystem (site + reviews + news + forums + Wikipedia, etc.)  [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html) |

In short: SEO tries to get you to position #1 in a list; GEO tries to get you into the *answer itself*. [forbes](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesagencycouncil/2026/09/23/the-death-of-rank-1-why-cmos-need-a-generative-engine-optimization-strategy/)

## Why GEO matters now

- More searches are ending in “zero‑click” AI answers, where users get a summary without visiting any site. [blog.hubspot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/generative-engine-optimization)
- AI models pull from many sources (your site, third‑party reviews, news, Q&A sites) to construct answers, so your brand narrative is distributed, not just on your domain. [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)
- Being cited in AI answers influences discovery, trust, and purchase decisions even when click‑throughs drop. [searchengineland](https://searchengineland.com/what-is-generative-engine-optimization-geo-444418)

## Core pillars of GEO

Practitioners typically break GEO into four areas: [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)

1. **Technical foundation**
   - Ensure AI crawlers (e.g., GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Googlebot) can access your content.
   - Use and configure `robots.txt`, sitemaps, and emerging AI‑specific files (e.g., `llm.txt`) so your pages are ingestible and governed correctly. [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)

2. **Structured data & semantics**
   - Add schema markup (JSON‑LD) to expose entities, relationships, products, FAQs, reviews, and organisational info in a machine‑readable way. [blog.hubspot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/generative-engine-optimization)
   - Make it easy for models to “understand” what your brand, products, and claims actually are.

3. **Content designed for AI retrieval**
   - Write clear, factual, well‑structured content with headings, bullet points, definitions, and direct answers to common questions. [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)
   - Emphasise E‑E‑A‑T signals (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) and keep information up to date. [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)
   - Use natural‑language phrasing that matches how people ask questions, not just short keyword strings. [blog.hubspot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/generative-engine-optimization)

4. **Brand narrative across the ecosystem**
   - Manage how your brand appears on review sites, news outlets, Wikipedia, Reddit, Quora, and other forums, because AI models learn from all of these. [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)
   - Ensure consistent messaging, correct facts, and positive, accurate representations wherever your brand is discussed. [seerinteractive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/what-is-generative-engine-optimization-geo)

## Related terms

GEO overlaps with several similar concepts, often used interchangeably in practice: [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_engine_optimization)

- Answer Engine Optimisation (AEO)
- Large Language Model Optimisation (LLMO)
- Artificial Intelligence Optimisation (AIO)
- AI SEO / Generative AI SEO

As of early 2026, there’s no single agreed academic definition separating these; in industry usage they generally point to the same goal: optimising for AI‑generated answers rather than link lists. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_engine_optimization)

Q5 How does the GEO Stack work?

If by **GEO Stack** you mean the **Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) Stack** used for improving visibility in AI search systems such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews, it’s essentially a **layered model for getting content retrieved and cited by AI systems**.  The GEO Lab+1

 ### The basic idea

 Traditional SEO asks:

 > **“How do I get this page to rank?”**

 GEO asks:

 > **“Once an AI system is answering a question, how do I make my content likely to be retrieved, understood, and cited?”**

 The important distinction is that AI search often works with **passages/sections of pages**, rather than simply choosing an entire page because it ranks highly.  The GEO Lab

 ### The five layers

 The GEO Lab's current framework has five main layers, preceded by a technical prerequisite:

 ### The basic idea

  > **“Once an AI system is answering a question, how do I make my content likely to be retrieved, understood, and cited?”**

 1. **Retrieval Probability**\
    Can the AI system find and select your content for the question?\
    This depends partly on the underlying search/indexing ecosystem. The framework therefore treats conventional SEO as a prerequisite: if your page isn't in the relevant candidate pool, the later GEO work has little opportunity to operate.  The GEO Lab+1
2. **Extractability**\
    Can the system easily understand and extract a useful passage?\
    Direct, declarative statements tend to be easier to reuse than vague introductions, long narrative passages, or information buried in prose. The optimisation target becomes the **individual section**, not merely the page.  The GEO Lab+1
3. **Entity Reinforcement**\
    Does the content consistently identify the people, companies, products, concepts, etc. it's talking about?\
    Consistent naming and clear relationships help an AI system understand _what_ an entity is and _which entity_ different references refer to.  The GEO Lab
4. **Structural Authority**\
    Does the content have signals that make it a credible source?\
    This includes things such as supporting evidence, citations, expertise indicators, and a structure that makes claims and their provenance clear.  The GEO Lab
5. **System Memory**\
    Does the entity/content become reinforced across the broader information ecosystem over time?\
    The idea is that repeated, consistent references across trustworthy sources can make an entity easier for AI systems to recognise and associate with particular topics.  The GEO Lab+1

 There's also a **Layer 0 / infrastructure gate**: crawlers need to be able to access, crawl and index the material in the first place.  The GEO Lab

 ### Think of it as a funnel

```
Can the system access it?
        ↓
Can it retrieve it?
        ↓
Can it extract the useful passage?
        ↓
Does it understand the entities?
        ↓
Does it have reasons to trust the source?
        ↓
Does repeated evidence reinforce the association?
        ↓
        CITATION / MENTION
```

 The reason for the ordering is important: **fixing a higher layer can't compensate for failure at a lower one**. If an AI system never retrieves your page, making the prose more authoritative won't help.  The GEO Lab

 ### A concrete example

 Suppose you run a company selling **project-management software**.

 A conventional SEO approach might create a page targeting:

 > “best project management software”

 A GEO-oriented approach would additionally create highly extractable sections such as:

 > **What is \[Product\]?**\
>  \[Product\] is a project-management platform designed for...

 > **Who is \[Product\] best suited for?**\
>  \[Product\] is designed primarily for...

 > **\[Product\] vs \[Competitor\]**\
>  \[Product\] differs from \[Competitor\] primarily in...

 Those passages give an AI system relatively self-contained pieces of information that can be incorporated into an answer. You would then reinforce the same entity description through structured data and credible third-party references.

 ### The really important takeaway

 **GEO isn't simply “SEO for ChatGPT.”** It's better understood as an additional layer on top of search visibility.

 A useful mental model is:

 **SEO gets your content into the pool → GEO makes individual passages useful candidates for an AI-generated answer.**  The GEO Lab

 There are, however, **several competing definitions of “GEO Stack”** in the industry; the five-layer model above is specifically the framework developed by The GEO Lab, rather than a universally standardised industry architecture.  The GEO Lab+1

What is System Memory in GEO?


In **Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)**, **System Memory** refers to the persistent associations an AI system builds between a brand, website, entity, and particular topics over time. It is essentially whether an AI repeatedly “remembers” your organization as a credible source for a specific subject. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

## How it works

System Memory develops when your site consistently demonstrates:

- Stable naming for your brand, products, and key entities.
- A clear, coherent site structure.
- Regularly updated content focused on a defined topic area.
- Multiple related pages reinforcing the same expertise.
- External citations, mentions, and links from other credible sources. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/system-memory/)

For example, if a company consistently publishes authoritative material about renewable-energy tax credits, uses the same entity names, updates its guidance, and is cited by reputable sources, AI systems may increasingly associate that company with renewable-energy tax expertise.

## Why it matters

Strong System Memory can make a site a persistent reference point in AI-generated answers—not merely a page that happens to rank for one query. It supports repeated retrieval and citation when users ask related questions. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

It is generally considered the fifth and highest layer of the GEO Stack, following:

1. Retrieval Probability.
2. Extractability.
3. Entity Reinforcement.
4. Structural Authority.
5. System Memory. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

System Memory is not a literal memory file that marketers can directly edit, and it is not guaranteed that an AI model will retain a site across conversations. Rather, it is a conceptual GEO term for the cumulative effect of consistent, reliable signals over time. Its main practical implication is to avoid inconsistent branding, fragmented site architecture, abrupt topic changes, and outdated content.

How to measure AI citation rate?

AI citation rate is the percentage of AI-generated answers (across a fixed set of test queries) that cite or mention your brand or link to your domain as a source.  It’s now a core visibility metric for brands in AI search, alongside “visibility rate” (any mention) and “share of voice” (your citations vs competitors’). [siftly](https://siftly.ai/blog/tools-measure-citation-rates-ai-generated-content-brands-2026)

## Core definition and formula

The standard definition used by most 2026 guides is:

- **AI citation rate** = (number of test queries where your brand/domain is cited or mentioned in the AI answer) ÷ (total number of test queries) × 100%. [siftly](https://siftly.ai/blog/tools-measure-citation-rates-ai-generated-content-brands-2026)

Some teams distinguish:

- **Mention/visibility rate**: your brand name appears (with or without a link). [discoveredlabs](https://discoveredlabs.com/blog/aeo-performance-metrics-what-to-measure-and-how-to-track-ai-citations)
- **Citation rate (strict)**: your URL appears in the sources panel or inline as a linked reference. [freecodecamp](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/how-to-measure-your-ai-citation-rate-across-chatgpt-perplexity-and-claude/)

Both use the same basic structure: count “successes” over a fixed query set, then convert to a percentage. [useomnia](https://www.useomnia.com/blog/ai-citation-tracking)

## Step‑by‑step measurement workflow

A practical, widely recommended method is a fixed‑query, multi‑platform audit:

1. **Build a fixed query set (15–30 questions)**  
   Include:
   - Brand queries: “What does [YourBrand] do?”  
   - Market/scenario queries: “Best tools for [job]”, “How to solve [problem]”  
   - Comparison queries: “[YourBrand] vs [Competitor]”  
   Use real buyer questions from Search Console, sales calls, and support tickets. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

2. **Choose platforms**  
   At minimum: ChatGPT (with browsing), Perplexity, Google AI Overviews; often also Claude and Gemini. Citation behavior differs by engine, so measure each separately. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

3. **Run each query and record results**  
   For every query × platform:
   - Did the AI mention your brand? (Y/N)  
   - Did it cite a URL on your domain? (Y/N)  
   - Optional: position in answer, competitors mentioned, sentiment, accuracy of description. [useomnia](https://www.useomnia.com/blog/ai-citation-tracking)

   Best practice: run each prompt multiple times over several days and deduplicate (count a URL once per run even if it appears multiple times). [useomnia](https://www.useomnia.com/blog/ai-citation-tracking)

4. **Calculate rates**

   For a given platform and query set:

   \[
   \text{Citation rate} = \frac{\text{# queries where your domain was cited}}{\text{Total # queries tested}} \times 100
   \]

   Similarly for visibility/mention rate using brand mentions instead of URL citations. [siftly](https://siftly.ai/blog/tools-measure-citation-rates-ai-generated-content-brands-2026)

   Example: 20 queries on Perplexity, your domain appears in 4 answers → citation rate = 4/20 × 100 = 20%. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

5. **Track over time**  
   Repeat the same query set monthly (same wording, same platforms). A single snapshot is less useful than the trend: are you gaining or losing citations vs competitors? [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

## Optional advanced metrics

Once you have the basics, many teams add:

- **Share of voice (SOV)**: your citations ÷ total citations across all brands for that query set. [useomnia](https://www.useomnia.com/blog/ai-citation-tracking)
- **Mention‑to‑citation gap**: visibility rate − citation rate, showing how often you’re named but not linked. [useomnia](https://www.useomnia.com/blog/ai-citation-tracking)
- **Engine‑ and country‑specific rates**: break down by platform and locale to see where you’re strongest/weakest. [useomnia](https://www.useomnia.com/blog/ai-citation-tracking)

## What GEO is (and isn’t)

- **What it is:** Optimizing content so generative engines select, summarize, and cite you when answering queries—especially “best X,” “X vs Y,” and category questions where AI recommendations influence buyers. [searchengineland](https://searchengineland.com/what-is-generative-engine-optimization-geo-444418)
- **What it isn’t:** A universal recipe to “rank in ChatGPT,” nor a replacement for SEO. Most GEO tactics overlap heavily with traditional search optimization (structure, authority, citations, brand mentions). [verityscore](https://verityscore.io/en/blog/does-geo-work-45-studies-evidence-2026/)

- **Inline citations:** ~+30%  
- **Readability/fluency:** ~+22%  
- **Domain‑specific jargon:** ~+21%

What is Extractability in GEO?

In Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), **extractability** is the measure of how easily an AI system can isolate, parse, and reuse a specific piece of your content as a clean, standalone answer in a generated response.  It’s the structural property that determines whether retrieved content actually gets quoted, not just read. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)

## Where extractability fits in GEO

Extractability is **Layer 2 of the GEO Stack**, sitting between:

- **Layer 1 – Retrieval Probability**: whether AI crawlers can access and index your page.  
- **Layer 2 – Extractability**: whether a retrieved section can be cleanly lifted and used.  
- **Layer 3+ – Entity Reinforcement, Structural Authority, System Memory**: trust, freshness, and brand signals that sustain citations over time. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/10-extractability-signals-ai-citation/)

A page can be fully crawlable (high retrieval) but still rarely cited if its answers are buried, ambiguous, or dependent on surrounding context (low extractability). [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)

## What makes content “extractable”?

High-extractability sections tend to share these traits: [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)

- **Answer-first structure**: the key claim or direct answer appears in the first sentence of the section.  
- **Section independence**: the passage makes sense on its own, without needing prior paragraphs for context.  
- **Compression resistance**: the meaning survives being reduced to a single sentence or short quote.  
- **Explicit entity anchoring**: subjects are named clearly (e.g., “Acme Cloud Backup” instead of “this solution”), avoiding vague pronouns.  
- **Clear formatting signals**: question-style headings, short paragraphs, lists, tables, and bolded key terms that help models identify the answer unit. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/10-extractability-signals-ai-citation/)

Extractability is distinct from *citation-worthiness*: extractability asks “Can a machine cleanly lift this?”, while citation-worthiness adds “Should it?” based on authority and trust. A page can be highly extractable but not worth citing; low extractability, however, caps citation potential regardless of quality. [menra](https://www.menra.ai/glossary/extractability)

## Why it matters for AI visibility

Generative engines retrieve many pages but cite only a fraction. Extractability is the gap between being fetched and being quoted: if an AI can’t cleanly extract a discrete answer from your content, it will often prefer a competitor’s page that states the same information more clearly. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-glossary-2026/)

Optimizing for extractability is therefore one of the highest-leverage, immediately actionable steps in GEO: rewrite sections to be self-contained, answer-first, and explicitly anchored so AI systems can lift them confidently into responses. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

How does the GEO Stack work?

The GEO Stack (Generative Engine Optimization Stack) is a layered framework designed to systematically improve how often and how accurately AI systems—like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews—cite or recommend your content.  Rather than treating AI visibility as a single tactic, it breaks the problem into sequential layers where each higher layer depends on the one below it being solid. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

## Core idea: a bottom-up stack

Most versions of the GEO Stack describe 5 layers that map to how generative engines actually work: they must first find you, then read you, then trust you as an entity, then prefer you over alternatives, and finally keep you in mind over time. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

A common 5-layer model (The GEO Lab / SEO Smart / others) looks like this: [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

1. **Retrieval Probability** – Can the AI system find and index your content?  
   - Technical signals: crawlability, robots.txt allowing AI bots, sitemaps, fast loading, API access where relevant. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)
   - If AI can’t retrieve you, nothing else matters. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

2. **Extractability** – Can the AI parse and quote your content accurately?  
   - Clear structure: headings that match questions, direct answers near the top, FAQs, lists, tables. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)
   - Machine-readable formats: schema.org markup, `llms.txt`, markdown briefs, JSON indexes to help models “chunk” your content cleanly. [cloudnsite](https://cloudnsite.com/blog/how-we-built-our-geo-stack)

3. **Entity Reinforcement** – Does the AI recognize you as a coherent, trustworthy entity?  
   - Consistent brand/entity signals: canonical name, NAP (name/address/phone), social handles, `sameAs` in schema, Wikipedia/Wikidata presence, author profiles. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)
   - Dense, consistent entity linking across your site and the web helps the model build a stable “knowledge graph” of your brand. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

4. **Structural Authority** – Does the AI prefer your content over competitors when answering?  
   - Trust signals: high-quality backlinks, citations in authoritative sources, strong E‑E‑A‑T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness). [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)
   - Topical authority and citation patterns matter more than raw link counts; the model learns which sources it can rely on for specific topics. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/generative-engine-optimisation-guide/)

5. **System Memory** – Does the AI keep citing you over time and across sessions?  
   - Freshness and consistency: regular updates, versioned content, ongoing citations, stable entity signals. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)
   - Some systems maintain conversation history or source preferences; reliable, up‑to‑date sources are more likely to be “remembered.” [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/generative-engine-optimisation-guide/)

Because the stack is sequential, weaknesses in lower layers (e.g., poor crawlability or messy structure) cap the effectiveness of everything above them. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

## A more technical implementation view

Some practitioners describe the GEO Stack in terms of concrete site infrastructure that makes content usable by AI assistants: [cloudnsite](https://cloudnsite.com/blog/how-we-built-our-geo-stack)

- **Crawler access**: Explicitly allow AI bots in `robots.txt` (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, etc.). [cloudnsite](https://cloudnsite.com/blog/how-we-built-our-geo-stack)
- **`llms.txt`**: A markdown index at the root summarizing key pages and links for LLMs. [cloudnsite](https://cloudnsite.com/blog/how-we-built-our-geo-stack)
- **Machine-readable briefs**: Short, structured markdown files per product/service with positioning, deliverables, pricing posture, and canonical URLs. [cloudnsite](https://cloudnsite.com/blog/how-we-built-our-geo-stack)
- **Structured data + extractable prose**: Schema.org markup plus content written with question-style headings and immediate, quotable answers. [cloudnsite](https://cloudnsite.com/blog/how-we-built-our-geo-stack)
- **JSON/structured indexes**: A machine-readable aggregation of offerings and content for systems that prefer structured data over HTML. [cloudnsite](https://cloudnsite.com/blog/how-we-built-our-geo-stack)

This technical stack directly supports the conceptual layers above: it boosts retrieval, extractability, and entity clarity, which then feed into authority and memory. [cloudnsite](https://cloudnsite.com/blog/how-we-built-our-geo-stack)

## Alternative layer naming (same logic)

Other frameworks use slightly different labels but the same bottom-up logic, for example: [thatware](https://thatware.co/5-layer-geo-stack-ai-visibility-framework/)

In practice, the GEO Stack works by:

What is LLM readability?

**LLM readability** is a measure of how easily and accurately a large language model (or an AI retrieval system that uses LLMs) can parse, segment, understand, and cite a piece of content.  It is distinct from human readability, which focuses on how easy text is for people to read; LLM readability focuses on how “machine‑friendly” the text is for AI systems like ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google AI Overviews. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/ultimate-guide-for-llm-readability-optimization-and-better-chunk-relevance)

## Core idea

In practice, LLM readability describes how well content can be:

- Processed in small chunks (passages/sections) without needing surrounding context to make sense. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)
- Embedded into coherent vector representations that accurately capture each section’s meaning. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)
- Extracted, summarized, and cited by generative AI when answering user queries. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/ultimate-guide-for-llm-readability-optimization-and-better-chunk-relevance)

High LLM readability means the content is more likely to be retrieved and quoted by AI answer engines; low LLM readability means it may be ignored or misinterpreted even if it’s well written for humans. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/ultimate-guide-for-llm-readability-optimization-and-better-chunk-relevance)

## How it differs from traditional readability

Traditional readability metrics (Flesch–Kincaid, LIX, etc.) estimate grade level or reading ease for humans using features like sentence length and syllable count.  LLM readability, by contrast, is about: [ai.jmir](https://ai.jmir.org/2026/1/e77149)

- **Structural clarity**: clear headings, logical hierarchy, and self-contained sections. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/ultimate-guide-for-llm-readability-optimization-and-better-chunk-relevance)
- **Semantic precision**: explicit definitions, consistent entity names, minimal ambiguity or contradictions. [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-llm-readability-optimization-olaf-kopp--u3arf)
- **Chunk independence**: each passage should make sense on its own so embeddings and retrieval work well. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)
- **Extractability**: facts and answers formatted so models can easily pull them into summaries or citations. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/ultimate-guide-for-llm-readability-optimization-and-better-chunk-relevance)

Some researchers also use “readability metric” more narrowly to mean an LLM-based score that predicts how easy generated text is for a target human audience, but that is a different (though related) concept focused on NLG evaluation rather than retrieval/citation optimization. [futureagi](https://futureagi.com/glossary/readability-metric/)

## Why it matters

As search and Q&A increasingly rely on LLMs and retrieval-augmented generation, content that scores well on LLM readability is:

- More likely to appear in AI-generated answers and overviews. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/ultimate-guide-for-llm-readability-optimization-and-better-chunk-relevance)
- More likely to be cited as a source when models construct responses. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/ultimate-guide-for-llm-readability-optimization-and-better-chunk-relevance)
- Less likely to be misinterpreted or partially ignored due to ambiguous structure or context dependence. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)

In short, LLM readability is about designing and writing content so that AI systems can treat it as a clean, reliable “meaning graph” rather than just human-oriented prose. [ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/how-to-make-content-llm-readable/)

What is GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation)?

## What GEO means

**Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)** is the practice of improving your content and online presence so that AI-powered search and answer tools—such as ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity—are more likely to discover, understand, cite, or recommend your organisation. It is often described as the AI-era evolution of SEO. [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)

Traditional SEO aims to help a page rank highly among a list of search results. GEO aims to make information clear and authoritative enough to be selected and incorporated into an AI-generated answer.

## SEO vs. GEO

| Traditional SEO | GEO |
|---|---|
| Targets rankings in search-result pages | Targets mentions, citations, and recommendations in AI answers |
| Emphasises keywords, links, and technical performance | Emphasises clarity, authority, structure, context, and factual accuracy |
| Usually sends users to a webpage | May influence an answer even when users do not click through |
| Focuses mainly on your website and search engines | Also considers reviews, media, forums, directories, and other sources |

The terms **AEO** (Answer Engine Optimisation), **AIO** (AI Optimisation), and **LLMO** (Large Language Model Optimisation) are also used for closely related practices; there is not yet a universally agreed distinction between them. [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_engine_optimization)

## How GEO works

Common GEO activities include:

- Publishing accurate, up-to-date content that directly answers real questions.
- Using clear headings, concise explanations, FAQs, lists, and well-defined terminology.
- Demonstrating expertise and trust through author information, evidence, references, reviews, and reputable third-party coverage.
- Adding structured data, such as Schema.org markup, so machines can identify products, people, organisations, and relationships.
- Maintaining consistent information about the brand across the website, news coverage, review sites, directories, forums, and other online sources.
- Making the site technically accessible to search and AI systems through sound crawling, indexing, and sitemap practices. [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)

## Example

Suppose a company sells project-management software. SEO might help its article rank for **“best project-management tools.”** GEO would additionally aim to make the company’s product understandable and credible enough that an AI assistant includes it when answering:

> “What are the best project-management tools for a small remote team?”

That means clearly explaining who the product is for, its features, pricing, limitations, integrations, and how it compares with alternatives—preferably supported by trustworthy, independent sources.

GEO does not guarantee inclusion in AI answers, because generative systems use changing retrieval methods and multiple sources. It is best understood as a complement to strong SEO, useful content, digital PR, and reputation management—not a replacement for them.

What is Extractability in GEO?

AI Overview        In Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)—the practice of optimizing content to be selected and cited by AI answer engines like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity—Extractability is the measure of how easily an AI system can isolate, parse, compress, and reuse a section of your content without losing its core meaning.It represents Layer 2 of the "GEO Stack" (sitting right above retrieval probability). Even if an AI engine crawls and retrieves your web page as a source, your brand will remain invisible in the final generated response if the specific answer cannot be cleanly extracted.Why Extractability Matters to AI EnginesUnlike traditional search engines that rank an entire URL based on keywords, generative engines process content sequentially through a process called Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):Retrieval: The engine finds a pool of relevant web pages based on a query.Extraction: The AI parses those pages, scanning for discrete passages or data points that directly answer the prompt.Synthesis & Citation: The model compresses those extracted fragments into a single cohesive response and attributes a citation link to the source.If your content has poor extractability—such as heavy narrative fluff, buried points, or highly interdependent paragraphs—the AI "Judge" cannot cleanly isolate the information, and it will quote a competitor instead.Core Principles of Extractable ContentTo score highly in AI extractability, content must be optimized for machine interpretability rather than narrative storytelling. How does the GEO Stack work?

AI Overview
The GEO Stack works as a bottom-up, five-layer framework that optimizes content visibility and brand trust inside Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) systems like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews.
Each layer depends on the one below it. If a lower layer fails, it breaks everything above it, requiring a strict sequential approach to optimization.
The Five Layers of the GEO Stack
• Retrieval Probability (Foundation): Ensures AI crawlers and search indexes can physically find, crawl, and access your web pages and passages.
• Extractability: Structures your text so large language models can easily parse and digest specific paragraphs using clear layouts like Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF).
• Entity Reinforcement: Connects your brand, founders, and services consistently across the web so AI models correctly map them inside knowledge graphs.
• Structural Authority: Builds canonical source material, pillar pages, and documented expertise that prove your brand is a credible subject-matter expert.
• System Memory: Compounds your visibility over time so generative engines repeatedly reuse and cite your brand as an established industry standard.

Yes. I searched current product documentation from OpenAI, Google, and Perplexity, plus recent academic research on AI-search citation behavior.

 ## The short answer

 AI search engines generally **do not choose citations directly from the entire web**. The process is closer to:

 **User query → query expansion/fan-out → web retrieval → relevance ranking → source filtering/reranking → answer generation → citation attribution**

 The exact algorithms are proprietary, but the public evidence points to several recurring factors.

 ### 1\. Relevance to the specific question

 The first gate is whether a page actually helps answer the query.

 Google says its AI Search systems use **“query fan-out”**—breaking a question into multiple related searches—to find relevant sites. OpenAI similarly says ChatGPT Search can rewrite a user's question into multiple targeted queries and conduct additional searches after examining initial results.  blog.google+1

 This means a page doesn't necessarily need to rank highly for the user's _exact wording_. It can be retrieved because it answers one of the underlying sub-questions.

 ### 2\. Evidence that can actually support the generated answer

 This is becoming an important distinction from ordinary SEO.

 Recent research analyzing **21,143 citations across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews/Gemini, and Perplexity** found that pages with greater citation influence tended to be:

 - longer,
- more structured,
- semantically aligned with the query,
- rich in extractable evidence,
- and likely to contain things such as definitions, numbers, comparisons, and procedural information.  arXiv

 In other words, **being relevant isn't necessarily enough**. The system benefits from pages containing information it can confidently extract and use.

 ### 3\. Authority and source quality

 Traditional search signals still matter.

 Google describes information retrieval as matching users with the best available information and historically used signals such as links and anchor text to identify important pages. Its current AI Search documentation also emphasizes connecting users with **original, high-quality content**.  Google Research+1

 Research comparing generative search with conventional Google search has also found a strong preference among AI-search systems for **third-party/earned media and authoritative sources**, rather than relying primarily on brand-owned pages.  arXiv

 So, for example, an independent technical review, government document, academic paper, or established publication may be attractive as evidence because it provides information the model can use with relatively strong provenance.

 ### 4\. Semantic match, not just keywords

 AI retrieval is increasingly concerned with **meaning**, rather than simply matching words.

 A page might never use exactly the phrase in the user's query but still be retrieved because its content is semantically related.

 This is particularly important because AI systems can transform:

 > “How do AI search engines select content to cite?”

 into several more specific retrieval tasks, such as:

 - how generative search retrieves documents
- how citations are selected
- how RAG systems rank retrieved passages
- what makes a source authoritative
- how AI-generated answers attribute claims

 Google explicitly describes query fan-out as a way of searching more deeply for relevant sites.  blog.google

 ### 5\. Freshness

 For queries where information changes, recency becomes important.

 Perplexity says its search uses a live web index, while ChatGPT Search is designed to retrieve timely information rather than relying solely on the model's training data.  Perplexity AI+1

 This means a page can be highly authoritative but still lose out to a newer source when the query concerns something rapidly changing.

 ### 6\. The page needs to be _retrievable_

 There's a less glamorous but crucial factor: **can the search system actually access the content?**

 OpenAI, for example, says websites need to allow its **OAI-Searchbot** to crawl them to be eligible for inclusion in ChatGPT Search.  OpenAI Help Center

 So citation visibility has a technical prerequisite:

 **If the system can't crawl/retrieve the information, the quality of the information doesn't matter at that moment.**

 ### 7\. Different AI engines select different sources

 This is perhaps the most important finding if you're thinking about "AI SEO."

 There isn't one universal **AI citation algorithm**.

 Research comparing ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and other systems finds substantial differences in which domains they cite and how frequently they cite them. One 2026 analysis, for example, reports substantial source exclusivity between different AI systems.  Machine Relations

 So:

 > **Ranking well in Google ≠ being frequently cited by ChatGPT ≠ being frequently cited by Perplexity.**

 There is overlap, but each system has its own retrieval stack, indexes, ranking mechanisms, models, and citation behavior.

 That is a useful conceptual model for understanding AI search.

 Putting the documentation and research together, the strongest recurring characteristics are:

| --- | --- |

 **AI citation = retrieval + relevance + evidence quality + source characteristics + query context + engine-specific ranking.**

 And importantly, **citation frequency isn't the same thing as factual reliability**. OpenAI itself warns that search results and citations can occasionally be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect and recommends checking the underlying source.  OpenAI Help Center

 ## What is System Memory in GEO?

 **System Memory refers to the persistent association an AI/search system develops between a website, its entities, and particular topics over time.**

 In simpler terms:

 > **Does the AI increasingly “know” that your organisation is associated with a particular subject?**

 The idea is that repeated, consistent signals across a site can create a durable association between:

 **Your entity → your expertise/topic → your supporting content → authoritative references**

 The GEO Lab describes System Memory as a **lagging indicator**: it emerges from consistently doing the preceding GEO layers well rather than from a single optimisation tactic.  The GEO Lab

 ### The five GEO layers

 | Layer | Question |
| --- | --- |
| 1\. Retrieval Probability | Can AI find your content? |
| 2\. Extractability | Can AI cleanly understand/extract it? |
| 3\. Entity Reinforcement | Does AI consistently identify the entities involved? |
| 4\. Structural Authority | Does the site's architecture reinforce topical authority? |
| **5\. System Memory** | **Has that association become persistent over time?** |

 The GEO Lab+1  ### How System Memory is supposedly built

 The framework identifies several mechanisms:

 - **Consistent entity naming** — using the same canonical name and terminology across your site.
- **Cross-page reinforcement** — multiple related pages consistently supporting the same topic/entity relationship.
- **Consistent publishing** — continuing to reinforce the site's topical focus over time.
- **Coherent site architecture** — hub-and-spoke structures and internal links that reinforce relationships between topics.
- **Avoiding contradictions** — different pages shouldn't give conflicting descriptions of the same entity or concept.  The GEO Lab+1

 The GEO Lab also argues that **System Memory cannot simply be “turned on” with a one-off change**; it accumulates from the preceding layers over time.  The GEO Lab

 ## An important caveat

 There is a distinction worth making here.

 **“System Memory” is not a universally established technical term for how ChatGPT, Google, Perplexity, or other AI systems work.** The terminology comes from **The GEO Lab's proprietary GEO Stack framework**. Its definition is therefore best understood as a **GEO practitioner framework/hypothesis**, rather than an established industry-standard mechanism inside AI models.

 In fact, The GEO Lab uses somewhat different wording across its pages: one definition describes System Memory as accumulated entity/topic associations, while another describes it specifically in terms of incorporation into an AI system's **parametric knowledge**.  The GEO Lab+1

 That distinction matters because modern AI search systems can retrieve information dynamically through search/RAG rather than relying solely on what a model has stored in its parameters.

 - “Acme Analytics — supply-chain forecasting”

 **Acme Analytics ↔ supply-chain forecasting**

 increasingly strong and persistent in AI/search systems.

 So when someone asks an AI system about _companies specialising in supply-chain forecasting_, Acme may have a greater chance of being associated with that topic—although **that outcome should not be treated as guaranteed**, and the underlying mechanism varies by AI/search platform.

search the web for: How to measure AI citation rate?

I searched the web for current guidance on **how to measure AI citation rate**. The terminology is not completely standardized, but there is a fairly clear measurement framework emerging.

 ## The core metric

 The cleanest definition I found is:

 > **AI Citation Rate = AI responses that cite your domain ÷ total valid AI responses × 100**

 For example, if you test 50 prompts and your website is cited in 18 valid responses:

 **18 ÷ 50 × 100 = 36% citation rate.**  Maxlytics+1

 The important point is that **citation rate measures whether your site was actually used as a source**, not merely whether your brand was mentioned. A brand can be mentioned without being cited.  Kompozy

 ## A practical measurement methodology

 I'd structure the measurement around five things:

 1. **Create a fixed prompt set**
   - e.g. 50–200 questions relevant to your category.
   - Include informational, commercial, comparison and problem-solving queries.
   - Keep the same prompts between measurement periods so changes are meaningful.  Maxlytics+1
2. **Run them across the AI platforms you care about**
   - ChatGPT
   - Google AI Overviews / AI Mode
   - Perplexity
   - Gemini
   - Claude, where relevant
3. **Record each answer individually**
    At minimum, capture:
   - Was your brand mentioned?
   - Was your domain cited?
   - Which URL was cited?
   - Which competitors were cited?
   - Where did the citation appear?
   - Was the brand recommended, merely mentioned, or used as supporting evidence?
4. **Calculate citation rate separately by platform**
    For example:
    | Platform | Prompts | Cited | Citation rate |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | ChatGPT | 100 | 32 | 32% |
   | Perplexity | 100 | 47 | 47% |
   | Gemini | 100 | 29 | 29% |
   | Google AI | 100 | 41 | 41% |
   This is more informative than collapsing everything into one number.
5. **Repeat the measurement**
    AI responses aren't deterministic, so a one-off test can be noisy. Current measurement methodologies recommend repeated runs and a fixed protocol rather than treating a single dashboard score as authoritative.  Maxlytics+1

 ## Don't confuse these three metrics

 This is probably the most important finding from the search.

 ### 1\. Citation rate

 **"How often does AI cite my website?"**

 $$
\frac{\text{responses citing my domain}}{\text{total valid responses}}
$$

 This is the metric I'd call **AI citation rate**.  Maxlytics+1

 ### 2\. Mention / visibility rate

 **"How often does AI mention my brand?"**

 A response can mention your company without citing your website.

 So:

 **Mention rate ≠ Citation rate**

 ### 3\. Citation share / share of voice

 **"Of all citations going to the competitive set, what proportion goes to me?"**

 For example, if your domain receives 40 citations and competitors receive 160:

 $$
40/(40+160)=20\%
$$

 This answers a different question from citation rate. Several sources explicitly warn that these denominators are often conflated.  Quattr+1

 ## Add "citation prominence"

 A simple citation rate can hide important differences.

 Suppose two sites both have a **30% citation rate**. One might be cited as the primary source for an answer, while the other is buried among six sources.

 So I'd additionally record:

 - **Citation position** — first / early / middle / late
- **Number of citations** in the answer
- **Whether your page is the primary supporting source**
- **URL-level citation frequency**
- **Recommendation status** — cited only vs mentioned vs recommended

 This distinction is also reflected in current AEO measurement frameworks, which separate citation frequency from prominence and recommendation.  Answer Engines Optimization+1

 As of **August 31, 2026**, Google says its Search Console **Generative AI performance report** has rolled out worldwide. It reports impressions from **AI Overviews and AI Mode**, including which pages receive those impressions and breakdowns by country, device and date.  Google Help

 That's useful, but it isn't the same thing as cross-platform citation rate. Search Console tells you about **Google's generative search features**; it doesn't give you a universal ChatGPT/Claude/Perplexity citation-rate measurement. Google's own documentation also cautions against third-party tools claiming access to Google's internal AI/ranking metrics.  Google for Developers

 ### My recommended KPI stack

 If you're building an **AI visibility measurement system**, I'd use:

 **Competitive**

 > **"Can I optimize my website and make an AI search engine discover it organically?"**

- changing headings
- adding statistics
- making paragraphs more "AI-readable"
- repeating entities
- adding specific phrases
- restructuring pages

## What is “Extractability” in GEO?

 In **Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)**, _extractability_ generally means **how easily an AI search system can identify, isolate, understand, and reuse a specific piece of information from a webpage when constructing an answer**.

 The concept is becoming more prominent in 2026 GEO frameworks, although it is **not a universally standardized academic metric**. The original GEO research established GEO as optimizing content for visibility in generative-engine responses; newer practitioner frameworks have subsequently broken that process into stages such as retrieval, extraction, and citation.  arXiv+1

 ### A simple way to think about it

 Traditional SEO asks:

 > **Can Google find and rank this page?**

 GEO asks something closer to:

 > **Can an AI find this information, understand it, and confidently use/cite it in its answer?**

 Extractability is the middle step:

 **Retrieval → Extractability → Citation/Use**

 A page can therefore be **retrieved but not effectively extracted**. For example, imagine a page contains:

 > “Although several approaches have historically been used, and while there are exceptions depending on the market, the company introduced its current pricing structure after…”

 An LLM may have difficulty determining the precise fact being asserted.

 Compare that with:

 > **The company introduced its current pricing structure in 2024.**

 The second statement is much easier to isolate and reuse without the surrounding paragraph.

 The GEO Lab currently defines extractability in essentially these terms: whether an AI system can isolate and reuse a section without losing its meaning. Its framework identifies **answer-first structure, section independence, compression resistance, and explicit entity anchoring** as important characteristics.  The GEO Lab+1

 ### What makes content highly extractable?

 Based on the current literature and practitioner frameworks, several patterns recur:

 - **Answer-first writing:** Put the actual answer or claim near the beginning of the section.
- **Self-contained sections:** A section should make sense without requiring three preceding paragraphs.
- **Explicit subjects/entities:** Say “OpenAI” rather than “the company” when the identity matters.
- **Clear headings:** Headings should accurately describe the information underneath them.
- **Atomic claims:** Break complicated ideas into discrete, independently understandable statements.
- **Compression resistance:** The core meaning should survive if an AI compresses a paragraph into one sentence.
- **Evidence close to the claim:** Put supporting facts, sources, dates, statistics, etc. near the assertion they support.

 The Prime Sentia GEO framework similarly describes extractability as structuring content into “atomic” passages that a model can lift as a whole, with answer-first passages and appropriate section length among its signals.  geo.primesentia.ai

 ### Extractability isn't the same as retrieval

 This distinction is important.

 Suppose you have a page containing:

 **Retrieval:** AI search successfully finds your webpage.

 **Extractability:** The AI can find the _relevant passage_ and understand what it says independently.

 **Citability:** The system actually uses that passage as a source in its answer.

 So:

 > **A page can be highly retrievable but poorly extractable.**

 That's one of the key ideas behind newer GEO frameworks.  The GEO Lab+1

 ### Is “extractability” an established scientific GEO metric?

 **Not yet in the same sense as established SEO metrics.**

 This is an important caveat. The original GEO paper by Aggarwal et al. formalized GEO as optimizing visibility in generative-engine responses, but it did **not establish “extractability” as a universally accepted standalone metric**.  arXiv

 More recent frameworks—particularly The GEO Lab's five-layer model—have operationalized extractability as a distinct concept. A 2026 survey of GEO research also notes that terminology and metrics across the field remain heterogeneous.  arXiv

 So I'd treat **“extractability” as an emerging GEO concept/framework rather than a settled industry-standard metric**.

 ### The practical GEO takeaway

 If you're optimizing content for ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, AI Overviews, etc., think:

 > **Don't just make the page readable. Make the individual facts and explanations easy for an AI to lift without changing their meaning.**

 For example:

 **Low extractability**

 > There are many factors that can affect pricing, and these have changed over time. Depending on the plan and circumstances, customers may encounter different costs…

 **Higher extractability**

 > **The product costs $49 per month on the standard plan.** Enterprise pricing is customized and requires contacting sales.

 The second version gives an AI a **clean, self-contained, attributable proposition** that can be incorporated into an answer with much less interpretation.

 One interesting development is that current GEO research is moving toward treating the **section or passage—not the entire webpage—as the unit that gets selected and potentially cited**.  The GEO Lab+1

If by **“the GEO Stack”** you mean the **Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) framework from The GEO Lab**, it’s a model for understanding how content gets found, extracted, trusted, and eventually cited by AI search systems such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews.  The GEO Lab+1

 ## How the GEO Stack works

 The key idea is that **AI visibility is a pipeline, not a single ranking factor**. The GEO Stack breaks that pipeline into sequential layers: if you fail at an earlier layer, improving a later one has limited value.  The GEO Lab

 ### 0\. Infrastructure / eligibility

 Before the five main layers, there is effectively an entry gate.

 Your content needs to be:

 - crawlable and accessible to search/AI systems
- indexed
- sufficiently competitive in traditional search to enter the relevant candidate pool

 The GEO Lab describes this as the prerequisite between conventional SEO and GEO. In its current framework, it calls this **Infrastructure Accessibility**, while another version describes indexed/top-30 ranking as **Stage 0**.  The GEO Lab+1

 So, conceptually:

 **SEO gets the content into the pool → GEO influences what happens after retrieval.**

 ### 1\. Retrieval Probability

 This asks:

 > **When an AI system is looking for an answer to a particular question, how likely is my content to be retrieved?**

 The focus is on things such as:

 - semantic alignment with the query
- clear topical focus
- entity/query matching
- section-level relevance
- avoiding content that is too broad or ambiguous

 A page can be excellent and still fail here because the relevant passage isn't selected for the AI system's candidate set.  The GEO Lab+1

 ### 2\. Extractability

 Once retrieved, the next question is:

 > **Can the AI system easily understand and reuse the relevant passage?**

 This is where content structure becomes important.

 For example, compare:

 > “There are several factors that businesses might want to consider when evaluating…”

 with:

 > **“GEO improves AI-search visibility by making content easier to retrieve, extract and cite.”**

 The second formulation gives an AI system a much cleaner informational unit to extract.

 The GEO Lab's experimental work specifically reports a higher citation rate for more declarative structures, although its reported experiment was small and the statistical result was not conventionally significant (p = 0.07).  The GEO Lab+1

 ### 3\. Entity Reinforcement

 Now the system needs to understand **who/what the content is actually about**.

 This layer focuses on consistent entity identification:

 - company names
- products
- people
- organisations
- concepts
- relationships between entities

 For example, if your website alternately calls something:

 > “Acme Analytics”\
>  “Acme”\
>  “our analytics platform”\
>  “the platform”

 an AI system has more work to do establishing that these references represent the same entity.

 Consistent naming and relationships create what the GEO Stack calls **entity reinforcement / entity gravity**.  The GEO Lab+1

 ### 4\. Structural Authority

 At this stage, the question becomes:

 > **Why should the AI system regard this information as trustworthy and authoritative?**

 Signals can include:

 - credible sources and citations
- expertise indicators
- authoritative references
- consistent information across the web
- appropriate structured data
- evidence supporting claims

 This is where GEO overlaps heavily with conventional SEO, digital PR, reputation and authority-building, but the objective is different: you're trying to establish information that an AI system can confidently incorporate into an answer.  The GEO Lab+1

 ### 5\. System Memory

 The highest layer concerns **accumulated recognition over time**.

 The idea is that repeated, consistent signals across your site and the wider web can cause AI systems to develop a stronger association between an entity and particular topics.

 For example:

 **Company X → cybersecurity compliance → enterprise SaaS → SOC 2**

 If that relationship is repeatedly reinforced through authoritative, consistent sources, the entity becomes more strongly associated with those concepts.

 The GEO Lab describes this as **System Memory**, and explicitly treats it as an emerging/longer-term layer rather than something you can simply switch on with one page change.  The GEO Lab+1

 ## How this differs from traditional SEO

 A useful simplification is:

 | Traditional SEO | GEO |
| --- | --- |
| Optimises a page | Often optimises individual passages/sections |
| Goal: ranking | Goal: inclusion/citation in AI answers |

 There **isn't one universally accepted “GEO Stack.”** Different practitioners use the term for different architectures. For example, CloudNSite describes its own GEO stack around crawler access, `llms.txt`, machine-readable content, structured data and extraction-friendly prose, while other sources describe three-layer or seven-layer models.  cloudnsite.com+1

## GEO vs traditional SEO — what actually changed?

 I searched current guidance from Google, Microsoft/Bing, OpenAI, and the original GEO research. The short version is:

 > **GEO isn't a replacement for SEO. It's a new optimization layer on top of SEO, because search engines and AI assistants increasingly retrieve information to construct an answer rather than simply rank pages for a human to click.**

 Google explicitly says its generative search features still rely on core Search ranking and quality systems, while Bing describes the shift as moving from ranking **documents** toward retrieving **groundable information** that can support an AI-generated answer.  Google Developers+1

 ### The fundamental change

 | Traditional SEO | GEO / AI search |
| --- | --- |
| **Goal:** rank a page | **Goal:** become useful source material for an answer |
| Primary unit: the **page/document** | Primary unit: **extractable, supportable information** |
| User sees 10-ish results and chooses | AI synthesizes information from multiple sources |
| Keywords and queries are central | **Entities, concepts, questions and context** become more important |
| Success = rankings, impressions, clicks | Success also = **citations, mentions, inclusion and downstream actions** |
| Optimize for one query | Systems may expand a query into multiple related searches |
| Backlinks establish authority | Authority still matters, but **provenance and evidence** become more important |
| Content is primarily consumed by humans | Content must be readily interpretable by **retrieval/AI systems and humans** |
| CTR is a major outcome | A citation may produce value **without a traditional click** |

Microsoft's current explanation is particularly clear: traditional search asks _“which pages should a user visit?”_, whereas AI grounding asks _“what information can an AI system responsibly use to construct a response?”_  Bing Blogs

 ## 1\. Ranking isn't disappearing — but the unit of competition changed

 This is probably the most important misconception about GEO.

 Google's current documentation says **SEO remains relevant** because AI Overviews and AI Mode use Google's existing Search systems to retrieve relevant pages. Google specifically describes retrieval-augmented generation and query fan-out as part of the process.  Google Developers

 So the old model:

 **Query → ranked pages → click**

 is increasingly accompanied by:

 **Query → expanded queries → retrieval → synthesis → cited answer**

 That means your page can be valuable in two different ways:

 1. It can **rank** for a search.
2. Its information can be **retrieved and incorporated into an AI-generated answer**.

 Those aren't necessarily the same thing.

---

 ## 2\. Keywords matter less than "answerable information"

 Traditional SEO often starts with:

 > "What keyword should this page rank for?"

 GEO asks a slightly different question:

 > "What specific information would an AI system need in order to answer this question accurately?"

 That changes content structure.

 For example, instead of creating a 2,000-word page targeting:

 **"best CRM software"**

 you'd want the page to contain clearly extractable information such as:

 - who the product is for
- key capabilities
- limitations
- pricing
- integrations
- implementation requirements
- comparisons
- concrete examples
- evidence supporting claims
- dates/version information
- author/company expertise

 The underlying principle is **information density + clarity \+ provenance**, rather than simply repeating the target phrase.

 Bing explicitly describes the new unit of value as "groundable information": discrete facts that can be supported and traced to a source.  Bing Blogs

---

 ## 3\. Content needs to be quotable/citable

 This is perhaps the biggest practical GEO change.

 A traditional SEO page can succeed by persuading someone to click through and read the whole thing.

 An AI system may instead extract **one paragraph, statistic, definition, comparison or claim**.

 So content benefits from making important claims:

 - explicit
- concise
- independently understandable
- supported by evidence
- attributable
- current

 Think:

 > **"What sentence from this page would I want an AI system to quote when answering a customer?"**

 rather than only:

 > "How do I get this page to rank?"

 This is closely aligned with Microsoft's current description of grounding, where source identification and attribution become important because multiple sources may be synthesized into a single answer.  Bing Blogs

---

 ## 4\. First-hand expertise becomes more strategically useful

 AI can generate generic explanations extremely cheaply.

 That makes generic content less differentiated.

 A page saying:

 > "There are many benefits of project management software..."

 doesn't give an AI much distinctive information.

 But:

 > "After migrating 240 projects from X to Y, we found that..."

 contains something much harder to manufacture generically.

 That's why Google's current AI-search guidance emphasizes **non-commodity content** alongside conventional SEO fundamentals.  Google for Developers

 This points toward:

 - original research
- proprietary data
- experiments
- case studies
- first-hand experience
- expert commentary
- unique comparisons
- original examples
- primary sources

 In other words, **being genuinely informative becomes a stronger moat.**

---

 ## 5\. "Entity clarity" matters more

 Traditional SEO has always cared about entities, but AI search makes the problem more obvious.

 An AI system needs to understand:

 **Who/what is this?\
 What does it do?\
 How is it related to other things?\
 What facts are associated with it?**

 For a company, for example, you want the web to consistently establish:

 > Company → product → category → customers → locations → people → competitors → evidence → authoritative sources.

 That makes structured data, consistent naming, clear site architecture and authoritative references useful beyond conventional rankings.

 But there's an important caveat: **don't confuse GEO folklore with confirmed ranking factors.**

 Google's current documentation explicitly says that many supposed special GEO techniques aren't necessary; its existing SEO best practices remain the foundation.  Google Developers

 ## 6\. Search queries themselves are becoming more complex

 Traditional search often looked like:

 > "best running shoes"

 AI search can turn that into something closer to:

 > "I'm training for my first marathon, I overpronate slightly, run mostly on pavement, have a £150 budget, and want something durable."

 The system can decompose that into multiple retrieval tasks.

 Google calls this **query fan-out**: generating related queries to retrieve additional information needed to answer the original request.  Google Developers

 This means content can gain visibility not only by matching the literal wording of a query, but by being useful to **the underlying information need**.

 That's a significant conceptual change.

 ## 7\. Links still matter — but citations become another visibility layer

 Traditional SEO largely asks:

 > "Where do I rank?"

 GEO adds:

 Microsoft explicitly says these metrics are designed to understand AI visibility rather than function as conventional ranking scores.  Bing Blogs+1

 That's a pretty strong indication of where measurement is heading.

 Here's an important commercial difference.

 Traditional SEO:

 **impression → SERP → click → website → conversion**

 AI search can become:

 **AI answer → brand exposure → citation → consideration → later conversion**

 Or:

 **AI answer → citation → click**

 Or even:

 **AI answer → no click, but brand remembered**

 So "organic traffic" becomes an incomplete measure of search visibility.

## What is LLM readability?

 I searched the web, and **“LLM readability” is an emerging term rather than a single standardized technical metric**. The common idea is:

 > **LLM readability is how easily an AI system—particularly an LLM working with retrieved passages—can parse, understand, extract, and accurately reuse information from content.**  Media Village+1

 It is related to **human readability**, but they aren't quite the same.

 ### Human readability vs. LLM readability

 | Human readability | LLM readability |
| --- | --- |
| Is it easy and pleasant for a person to read? | Can an AI reliably identify and interpret the information? |
| Emphasis on flow and comprehension | Emphasis on structure and extractability |
| Context can span several paragraphs | Individual passages should often make sense on their own |
| Pronouns can make prose natural | Explicitly naming entities can reduce ambiguity |
| Narrative prose can work well | Definitions, lists, tables and answer-first structures can be easier to extract |

For example:

 **Less LLM-readable:**

 > The company introduced this in 2024. It was subsequently expanded, and this approach has helped many businesses.

 An AI retrieving that paragraph separately may not know what **“the company,” “this,” “it,”** or **“this approach”** refers to.

 **More LLM-readable:**

 > **OpenAI introduced X in 2024. OpenAI expanded X in 2025. The X approach has helped businesses with Y.**

 The second version contains more explicit context, so a retrieved passage is less dependent on the surrounding article.

 ### What makes content LLM-readable?

 Across the sources I found, the recurring characteristics are:

 - **Clear headings** that accurately describe the section.
- **Direct answers and definitions**, rather than making the model infer the answer.
- **Self-contained passages** that remain understandable when separated from the rest of the page.
- **Consistent terminology** for people, companies, products and concepts.
- **Explicit relationships and context** instead of ambiguous pronouns.
- **Logical information hierarchy** using headings, paragraphs, lists and tables.
- **Factual precision and consistency**, with claims supported by appropriate context.
- **Accessible content**, so important information isn't hidden behind mechanisms an AI system cannot easily retrieve.  Media Village+2

 ### Why does it matter?

 The concept is particularly relevant to **AI search / Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)**.

 Modern AI search systems may retrieve individual passages from webpages rather than simply treating an entire webpage as one unit. Consequently, a page can be perfectly understandable when read from beginning to end while containing individual passages that are ambiguous when retrieved independently.  The GEO Lab+1

 So the practical goal isn't really:

 **“Write for robots instead of humans.”**

 It's closer to:

 **“Make every important piece of information unambiguous enough that both a human and an AI can understand it.”**

 One important caveat: **there is currently no universally accepted “LLM readability score.”** Some companies have developed their own frameworks or auditing tools, but the terminology and measurements vary.  Media Village+1

 ### One-sentence definition

 **LLM readability = the degree to which content is structured and written so that an LLM can independently retrieve, interpret, extract, and accurately represent its meaning.**

## What is “Retrieval Probability” in AI search?

 The phrase **retrieval probability** is currently being used in two somewhat different ways, and it’s important not to confuse them.

 ### 1\. In AI-search / GEO discussions

 In the newer **Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)** context, _retrieval probability_ means roughly:

 > **The likelihood that an AI search system will retrieve a particular piece of content as part of answering a user's query.**

 For example, suppose someone asks an AI search engine:

 > “What are the best ways to improve sleep quality?”

 The system might search its index and retrieve chunks from 20 different webpages. A particular paragraph on your website has some likelihood of being among those retrieved chunks.

 That likelihood is what GEO practitioners are calling **retrieval probability**. The important distinction is that **being highly ranked in conventional Google search doesn't necessarily mean being retrieved by an AI search system**. AI systems can retrieve passages or chunks rather than simply selecting the highest-ranked whole webpages.  The GEO Lab+1

 A simplified conceptual model is:

 **Query → retrieve candidate content → extract useful passages → generate answer → potentially cite source**

 If your content isn't retrieved in the first place, it generally cannot become part of the answer or citation.  The GEO Lab

 ### 2\. In technical RAG research

 There is also a more formal use of the term.

 In a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system, a retriever gives candidate passages a score based on their relationship to the query. Those scores can be converted into a probability distribution, for example:

 $$
P_R(c_i|q)=
\frac{e^{s(q,c_i)}}{\sum_j e^{s(q,c_j)}}
$$

 where:

 - $q$ = the query
- $c_i$ = candidate piece of content
- $s(q,c_i)$ = retrieval/similarity score
- $P_R(c_i|q)$ = the model's retrieval probability for that candidate

 The Vector Institute's FedRAG documentation uses precisely this formulation: retrieval probabilities are obtained by applying a softmax to retrieval scores.  vectorinstitute.github.io

 So in technical RAG research, **retrieval probability can be an actual mathematical quantity**.

---

 ## The important caveat

 The phrase **“Retrieval Probability” is not currently a single universally standardized AI-search metric**.

 This is particularly important if you've encountered claims such as:

 > “Your page has a 73% retrieval probability in ChatGPT.”

 An external SEO/GEO tool generally **cannot know the actual probability used internally by ChatGPT, Google, Perplexity, etc.** The underlying indexes, embeddings, ranking/reranking systems and retrieval policies are proprietary. One analysis specifically warns that external tools can estimate _retrieval-readiness signals_, but cannot observe the true internal retrieval probability.  Better Robots.txt

 There are, however, genuine research metrics with similar names. For example, a 2026 research paper introduces **Retrieval Probability Score (RPS)** for identifying entities that neural retrievers systematically fail to retrieve. It measures how consistently an entity appears in top-k retrieval results across relevant contexts.  arXiv

 So I'd distinguish them as:

 | Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Retrieval probability** in GEO | Estimated likelihood that AI search retrieves your content |
| **Retrieval probability** in RAG | Mathematical probability assigned to candidate retrieved contexts |
| **Retrieval Probability Score (RPS)** | A research metric for measuring an entity's retrievability |
| **Recall** | How much of the relevant information a retrieval system successfully retrieves |

### Why this matters for AI search

 The practical idea behind the newer GEO usage is quite useful even if the terminology isn't standardized:

 **Traditional SEO:**\
 `Query → rank webpages → user clicks`

 **AI search:**\
 `Query → query expansion/fan-out → retrieve passages → select/use passages → synthesize answer → cite sources`

 Consequently, optimizing a page merely to rank highly isn't necessarily sufficient. The content also needs to contain **clear, semantically specific passages that the retrieval system can match to the questions it is trying to answer**. Research on RAG systems likewise shows that neural retrievers can have “blind spots” where relevant information exists but has low retrievability because of how it is represented in embedding space.  arXiv

 **Bottom line:** _Retrieval probability_ is best understood as **“how likely is this piece of information to enter the AI's retrieved evidence set for a particular query?”** In GEO, it's primarily a conceptual/estimated metric; in RAG research, related retrieval probabilities can be mathematically defined and measured.  vectorinstitute.github.io+1

## What is GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation)?

 **Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)** is the practice of improving a website, brand, or piece of content so that **AI-powered search and answer systems are more likely to discover it, use it as a source, mention it, or cite it in their generated answers**.

 The term was formally introduced in a 2023 research paper, which described GEO as a way to increase a source's visibility in responses produced by generative search engines.  arXiv

 In practical terms:

 > **SEO tries to get your website ranked in search results. GEO tries to get your information represented in AI-generated answers.**

 ### Why GEO has emerged

 Traditional search engines generally return a list of links:

 **User → Google/Bing → search results → website**

 Generative search changes the experience:

 **User → AI search → synthesised answer → selected sources/citations**

 AI systems can retrieve information from multiple sources and synthesise it into a single response. Google's current AI-search documentation describes this using techniques including **retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)** and **query fan-out**.  Google for Developers

 That creates a new visibility question for businesses:

 **"When someone asks an AI about my industry, product, service, or problem, does the AI know about me—and does it use or cite my content?"**

 That's the problem GEO is intended to address.

 ## GEO vs SEO

 | Traditional SEO | GEO |
| --- | --- |
| Optimises for search engines | Optimises for generative/AI search experiences |
| Goal: rankings and organic clicks | Goal: visibility, mentions and citations in AI answers |
| Focuses heavily on keywords, links, technical SEO and search intent | Focuses additionally on information clarity, authority, entities, corroboration and extractability |
| User receives a list of results | User may receive a synthesised answer |
| Google/Bing rankings are a major measurement | AI citations, mentions and inclusion in generated answers become additional measurements |

There is an important caveat, though: **GEO isn't necessarily a replacement for SEO.**

 Google explicitly says that its generative AI search features are built on its existing Search ranking and quality systems, and that foundational SEO remains relevant. Google actually describes GEO/AEO as terminology for work focused on AI search, while saying that, from Google's perspective, this is still SEO.  Google for Developers

 ## What does GEO actually involve?

 The useful part of GEO is less about a secret technical trick and more about making information **easy for AI systems to find, understand, verify and confidently use**.

 Common areas include:

 - **High-quality, original content** — information that adds something beyond generic material already available online.
- **Clear answers** — directly answering the questions your audience actually asks.
- **Strong topical authority** — demonstrating genuine expertise around a subject rather than publishing isolated articles.
- **Clear entities and relationships** — making it unambiguous who your company is, what you offer, who your products/services are for, etc.
- **Evidence and corroboration** — claims that can be supported by credible sources and other independent references.
- **Good technical SEO** — crawlability, indexability, page structure and accessibility still matter.
- **Structured, readable content** — headings, tables, definitions, FAQs and concise explanations can make information easier to process, although Google says there is no special requirement to "chunk" content for AI.  Google for Developers

 An important distinction is that **some GEO advice circulating online is considerably more speculative than the underlying concept**. Google specifically says there is no need for special AI markup, `llms.txt`, artificially tiny content chunks, or inauthentic attempts to manufacture mentions for Google AI search.  Google for Developers

 ## An example

 Imagine you run a company selling accounting software.

 Someone asks an AI search engine:

 > "What are the best accounting software options for a small UK business?"

 A traditional SEO strategy might aim to rank your comparison page for:

 **"best accounting software UK"**

 A GEO strategy also asks:

 - Does the AI understand what our product actually does?
- Is our company consistently described across the web?
- Does our site clearly explain our features and limitations?
- Are independent publications discussing us?
- Are there authoritative sources that corroborate our claims?
- Is our information current?
- Can the AI retrieve useful passages from our pages?
- When the AI generates an answer, does it have sufficient evidence to mention or cite us?

 The objective isn't simply **"rank #1."**

 It's closer to:

 **"Become a credible, retrievable source that an AI can confidently incorporate into its answer."**

 ## The research behind the term

 The original 2023 GEO research is particularly interesting because the researchers actually tested optimisation techniques against generative engines. Their experiments reported visibility improvements of **up to 40%**, while also finding that effectiveness varied between domains.  arXiv

 That doesn't mean "GEO can increase your visibility by 40%" as a general business claim—the experiment was conducted under specific research conditions. But it established GEO as a measurable research problem rather than merely a marketing buzzword.

 ## Where GEO is heading

 GEO is increasingly being used to describe optimisation for several different AI-search environments, including:

 - Google's AI search experiences
- Microsoft's AI/Copilot ecosystem
- ChatGPT and other answer-oriented AI systems
- AI-native search engines such as Perplexity
- Other LLM-powered discovery experiences

 The terminology is still evolving. Microsoft, for example, now explicitly uses **"generative engine optimization (GEO)"** when discussing AI visibility and the ways brands can be discovered and recommended by AI systems.  Microsoft Advertising

 At the same time, Google is taking a more conservative position: **don't treat GEO as a completely separate discipline with magical AI-specific ranking hacks.** Its current guidance is essentially that strong SEO fundamentals, valuable original content and good technical accessibility remain the foundation.  Google for Developers+1

What is System Memory in GEO?

In Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO), **System Memory** is Layer 5 of the GEO Stack: it’s the long‑term, accumulated pattern of entity and topic associations that AI systems build about your site over time. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

## What System Memory means in GEO

System Memory is not a technical RAM setting; it’s a conceptual layer describing how generative AI “remembers” your brand as a stable, authoritative source on specific topics. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

- It reflects whether an AI has formed a **stable, repeated association** between your entity (brand, site, author) and a topic across many interactions and crawls. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)
- Sites with strong System Memory become **persistent reference points** that models default to when constructing answers in that domain. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/system-memory/)
- Unlike Layers 1–4 (Retrieval Probability, Extractability, Entity Reinforcement, Structural Authority), System Memory **cannot be directly engineered**; it emerges from sustained, consistent application of the lower layers over months or quarters. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

## How System Memory is built

System Memory accumulates through signals that reinforce a coherent, long‑term topical identity: [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

- **Consistent entity usage**: same naming conventions, canonical brand/entity mentions across content. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)
- **Structural coherence**: stable information architecture and URL structures over time. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)
- **Regular, on‑topic publishing**: ongoing content that repeatedly ties your entity to the same core topics. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)
- **Cross‑page reinforcement**: multiple related pages supporting the same entity–topic clusters. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)
- **External citations and freshness**: backlinks, mentions, and visible update cadence that signal ongoing relevance. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/system-memory/)

## Why it matters for GEO

GEO aims to make your content **retrievable, extractable, and citable** in AI‑generated answers. System Memory is the layer that determines whether your site is **remembered and preferred** over time, not just found once. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/generative-engine-optimisation-guide/)

- It helps turn one‑off citations into **durable authority**, where your brand is encoded as “the source” for certain problems or topics. [metaflow](https://metaflow.life/blog/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-search-engine-optimization)
- Inconsistent naming, structural rewrites, or sudden topic pivots can **degrade** System Memory by breaking the accumulated associations. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

How to measure AI citation rate?

AI citation rate is the percentage of AI-generated answers (across a defined set of queries and platforms) that cite your domain or URL as a source. [citability](https://citability.dev/blog/how-to-measure-ai-citation-rate)

## Core definition and formula

- **AI citation rate** = (Number of tracked queries where your URL/domain is cited as a source) ÷ (Total number of tracked queries) × 100. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)
- Some teams also define a related metric, **own-site citation share**, as your domain’s citations divided by all citations returned across the same query set. [babel42](https://babel42.io/blog/ai-citation-rate)
- Crucially, “citation” means the engine actually lists or links to your URL in its sources, not just mentions your brand in the prose. [babel42](https://babel42.io/blog/ai-citation-rate)

## Step-by-step: how to measure it manually

You can get a reliable baseline in under an hour with a simple fixed-query test. [citability](https://citability.dev/blog/how-to-measure-ai-citation-rate)

### 1. Build a fixed query set (15–30 questions)

Choose realistic buyer or user questions in your niche, avoiding overly branded terms. A good mix: [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

- Market-scenario queries: “What tools can do X?”, “Recommended solutions for Y?”
- Comparison queries: “A vs B for Z”
- A few brand-name queries to sanity-check coverage.

### 2. Pick platforms and lock conditions

Test across multiple AI search/answer engines because behavior differs: e.g., ChatGPT (with web search), Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

For each run, note:

- Engine and model/version (if visible)
- Whether web/search is on
- Country, language, logged-in status, and time. [deepsmith](https://deepsmith.ai/blog/measure-ai-citation-rate)

### 3. Run queries and record raw answers

For each query:

- Paste it into each engine.
- Save the full answer text and the list of sources/citations shown.
- Normalize source URLs (strip UTM params, etc.). [deepsmith](https://deepsmith.ai/blog/measure-ai-citation-rate)

### 4. Define what counts as a citation

Decide rules such as:

- Any URL on your domain in the sources panel or inline footnotes = citation.
- Brand mention without a link = visibility, not citation. [babel42](https://babel42.io/blog/ai-citation-rate)

Log for each run:

- `brand_citation`: Yes/No
- `citation_count`: number of distinct qualifying URLs from your domain
- `invalid_reason` if the run failed (e.g., blocked, error). [deepsmith](https://deepsmith.ai/blog/measure-ai-citation-rate)

### 5. Calculate your rate

For each engine (and optionally per query group):

\[
\text{AI citation rate} = \frac{\text{Valid runs with at least one brand citation}}{\text{Total valid runs}} \times 100
\]  [deepsmith](https://deepsmith.ai/blog/measure-ai-citation-rate)

Example: 20 queries × 3 engines = 60 runs. If your domain is cited in 9 runs, your overall citation rate is 9 ÷ 60 × 100 = 15%. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

## Optional: complementary metrics

To get more context, many teams track:

- **Visibility rate**: % of answers that mention your brand at all (with or without a link). [freecodecamp](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/how-to-measure-your-ai-citation-rate-across-chatgpt-perplexity-and-claude/)
- **Citation share (own-site citation share)**: your citations ÷ all citations returned across the query set. [babel42](https://babel42.io/blog/ai-citation-rate)
- **Prompt coverage**: % of your target queries that return *any* sourced answer on a given engine. [amplerank](https://amplerank.ai/how-to/measure-ai-citations)
- **Sentiment/accuracy**: whether the AI’s description of your brand is correct and positive. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

## Automation and tools

Manual testing works for baselines, but repeated measurement is usually automated:

- Use scripts or APIs to run your fixed query set on each engine on a schedule (e.g., weekly or monthly). [deepsmith](https://deepsmith.ai/blog/measure-ai-citation-rate)
- Or use dedicated AI citation tracking tools that handle query execution, source extraction, and dashboards. [siftly](https://siftly.ai/blog/tools-measure-citation-rates-ai-generated-content-brands-2026)

Whatever method you choose, keep the query set, platforms, and measurement rules consistent so you can track trends over time rather than relying on a single snapshot. [mlytics](https://www.mlytics.com/blog/ai-citation-rate/)

Does GEO actually work?

Yes—**GEO (generative engine optimization) can work**, but not as a reliable “hack” that makes ChatGPT or Google automatically recommend your brand. It is better understood as an extension of SEO: improving the likelihood that AI systems retrieve, trust, cite, and mention your content. [digiday](https://digiday.com/media/geo-hype-busted-experts-call-it-more-seo-than-new-discipline/)

## What the evidence shows

Controlled research has found that changes such as adding credible statistics, expert quotations, citations, and clearer writing can increase a source’s visibility in generative-engine responses—sometimes by up to roughly 40%. However, results vary by topic and platform, and those figures refer to visibility in answers, not necessarily traffic, leads, or sales. [digitalagencynetwork](https://digitalagencynetwork.com/aeo-vs-seo-vs-geo/)

A recent review of 45 studies reached a more cautious conclusion: GEO has measurable effects, particularly after a page is already retrieved, but no stable, cross-platform formula has been shown to turn page edits consistently into more organic traffic or revenue. [verityscore](https://verityscore.io/en/blog/does-geo-work-45-studies-evidence-2026/)

## What tends to work

- Publishing genuinely useful, specific, factually supported content.
- Demonstrating first-hand expertise and clearly identifying authors or sources.
- Using descriptive headings, direct answers, definitions, tables, and concise explanations.
- Including verifiable statistics and linking to authoritative references.
- Earning independent mentions, reviews, and citations from reputable websites.
- Maintaining strong technical SEO so search and AI systems can discover the content.

These are largely good SEO and editorial practices already—not secret formatting tricks. [digiday](https://digiday.com/media/geo-hype-busted-experts-call-it-more-seo-than-new-discipline/)

## What usually does not work

- Keyword stuffing or awkwardly inserting “AI-friendly” phrases.
- Publishing large volumes of generic, AI-generated articles.
- Treating one favorable ChatGPT response as proof of success.
- Assuming a citation automatically produces measurable conversions.
- Paying for a tool that reports “AI visibility” without connecting it to qualified visits, enquiries, or revenue.

Visibility can also fluctuate substantially: one industry guide reports that 40–60% of cited sources may change from month to month. [searchengineland](https://searchengineland.com/what-is-generative-engine-optimization-geo-444418)

## Practical verdict

GEO is worth testing if your customers ask AI tools for comparisons, recommendations, or research—especially in B2B, software, professional services, and other consideration-heavy markets. But it should complement, not replace, SEO, public relations, customer reviews, and conversion optimization.

Measure it by repeatedly tracking relevant prompts and then checking:

1. Whether your brand is mentioned.
2. Whether the mention is accurate and favorable.
3. Which pages or third-party sources are cited.
4. Referral traffic and assisted conversions.
5. Qualified leads or sales—not just “visibility.”

**Bottom line:** GEO works as source-selection and brand-visibility optimization, but claims of guaranteed rankings, instant traffic, or a universal AI-search formula are not supported by the evidence.

What is Extractability in GEO?

In Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), **extractability** is the structural property of a content section that determines whether an AI system can cleanly isolate, parse, and reuse it in a generated answer without losing its meaning.  It is distinct from crawlability or relevance: a page can be fully crawlable and highly relevant, yet still score zero on extractability if its key claims are buried in dependent prose rather than stated independently at the start of a section. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)

## Where extractability fits in GEO

Extractability is defined as **Layer 2 of the GEO Stack**, sitting between:

- **Layer 1 – Retrieval Probability**: whether AI systems can access and index your content. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)
- **Layer 2 – Extractability**: whether retrieved content can be quoted accurately and used directly. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)
- **Layer 3+**: entity reinforcement, structural authority, and system memory (trust, freshness, etc.). [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

In practice, this means: even if your page is retrieved by an AI model, poor extractability often leads the model to skip your content and cite a competitor that answers the same question more cleanly. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/10-extractability-signals-ai-citation/)

## What makes content “extractable”?

High extractability correlates with specific structural patterns that make passages easy for AI to lift and reuse: [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)

- **Answer-first structure**: the main claim or direct answer appears in the first sentence of a section. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)
- **Section independence**: the passage makes sense on its own, without needing surrounding context. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)
- **Compression resistance**: the meaning survives being reduced to a single sentence or short quote. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)
- **Explicit entity anchoring**: subjects are named clearly (e.g., “Acme Cloud Backup” instead of “our solution” or “it”). [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)
- **Clear formatting signals**: question-style headings, short paragraphs, bulleted/numbered lists, and bolded key terms. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-glossary-2026/)
- **Schema-content alignment**: structured data (e.g., FAQ, HowTo, Product schema) matches the on-page content. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/10-extractability-signals-ai-citation/)
- **Explicit numbers and units**: concrete values (“48-hour RTO”, “£1,200/month”) instead of vague language. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/10-extractability-signals-ai-citation/)

These patterns help AI systems treat a passage as a **quote-ready block** rather than ambiguous prose. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/extractability/)

## Why extractability matters for visibility

Extractability is effectively the gate between “being read” and “being cited”:

- AI models retrieve many pages but quote only a fraction. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/10-extractability-signals-ai-citation/)
- Extractability measures the likelihood that, once retrieved, your content will be the source the model actually uses. [menra](https://www.menra.ai/glossary/extractability)
- Low extractability caps your citation rate regardless of how authoritative or relevant your content is. [menra](https://www.menra.ai/glossary/extractability)

For businesses, this means optimizing for extractability is as important as optimizing for relevance: it’s about making your answers **machine-quotable**, not just human-readable. [acromatico](https://acromatico.com/glossary/extractability)

How does the GEO Stack work?

The **GEO Stack** usually refers to a five-layer framework for **Generative Engine Optimisation**—making a website or organisation more likely to be found, understood, trusted, and cited by AI search systems such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and AI Overviews. It works from the bottom up: weaknesses in an earlier layer limit the value of later layers. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/geo-stack/)

## The five layers

1. **Retrieval probability — can AI find you?**  
   Make content crawlable, indexable, technically accessible, and relevant to the questions users ask. This includes site architecture, crawler access, semantic relevance, and sometimes APIs or other data feeds. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

2. **Extractability — can AI understand and quote you?**  
   Present information in clear, self-contained units: descriptive headings, direct answers, short paragraphs, lists, tables, FAQs, and structured data. The goal is to give an AI system an unambiguous passage it can accurately reuse. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

3. **Entity reinforcement — does AI recognise who you are?**  
   Keep your organisation’s name, description, people, products, locations, and relationships consistent across your website and reputable external sources. Schema markup, author information, and consistent profiles help reinforce the entity. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

4. **Structural authority — does AI prefer you over alternatives?**  
   Build evidence that your information is reliable and authoritative. Typical signals include quality backlinks, independent citations, expert authorship, first-hand experience, and strong topical coverage. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

5. **System memory — does your visibility persist?**  
   Keep important information accurate and current through updates, consistent publishing, and change monitoring. Freshness and reliability can help maintain citations rather than producing a one-off appearance. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/field-manual/)

## How it operates in practice

The workflow is sequential:

**Make content discoverable → make it easy to extract → establish a clear entity → build authority → maintain freshness.**

For example, suppose a software company wants AI systems to recommend its project-management product:

- Its product pages must be crawlable and technically accessible.
- Each page should clearly state what the product does, who it serves, pricing or limitations, and relevant alternatives.
- The company name, product name, founders, and category should match across its site, directories, reviews, and media.
- Independent reviews and expert references should support its claims.
- Product details and comparison pages should be updated whenever features or pricing change.

A site with impressive backlinks but inaccessible or poorly structured product information may still be difficult for an AI system to cite. Conversely, well-structured content without independent authority may be easy to read but not sufficiently trusted.

## Important qualification

“GEO Stack” is not one universally standardised technology or protocol. Different agencies use the term for different models—some describe the five layers above, while others use a technical stack involving crawler permissions, `llms.txt`, machine-readable briefs, schema, and JSON indexes.  The shared idea is an end-to-end system for improving AI discoverability and citation, rather than a single optimisation trick. [cloudnsite](https://cloudnsite.com/blog/how-we-built-our-geo-stack)

GEO vs traditional SEO — what changed?

The biggest change is that optimization has shifted from ranking whole pages for clicks (traditional SEO) to earning citations inside AI‑generated answers (GEO). In practice, that means moving from “position #1 on a SERP” to “being the source the model chooses to quote or rely on.” [contently](https://contently.com/2026/02/11/geo-vs-traditional-seo/)

## What GEO is (and isn’t)

- **GEO = Generative Engine Optimization**: making content easy for AI answer engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, etc.) to understand, trust, and cite when synthesizing responses. [contently](https://contently.com/2026/02/11/geo-vs-traditional-seo/)
- It’s **not a replacement** for SEO; it’s an additional layer on top of core SEO fundamentals (quality content, technical health, authority). [thriveagency](https://thriveagency.com/news/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo/)

## Core differences: what actually changed

| Dimension | Traditional SEO | GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary goal** | Rank a link; earn the click | Be cited/quoted inside an AI answer (even if no click happens) |
| **Success metric** | Rankings, organic sessions, CTR, conversions | Citation share, AI referral traffic, brand mentions in AI answers |
| **Surface** | Google/Bing SERPs (blue links) | AI Overviews, chatbots, conversational search interfaces |
| **Unit of optimization** | Whole pages/posts | Extractable passages, stats, tables, definitions |
| **Query style** | Keyword phrases with known volume | Concepts expressed in many conversational ways |
| **Ranking signals emphasis** | Backlinks, keywords, page speed, UX | Clarity, structured data, authoritative stats, consistent entities, recency |
| **Control over presentation** | Meta title/description, snippet | AI decides wording; you influence via structure, schema, and clarity |
| **Result dynamics** | Relatively stable rankings | Answers can vary by query, time, and model state |

Sources for these contrasts: [contently](https://contently.com/2026/02/11/geo-vs-traditional-seo/)

## Why this matters for strategy

- **From “click” to “citation”**: In traditional SEO, success is a user clicking your link. In GEO, success can be the model using your data or quote in its answer, which may or may not drive a click. [contently](https://contently.com/2026/02/11/geo-vs-traditional-seo/)
- **From keywords to concepts**: Instead of targeting a fixed set of high‑volume keywords, GEO requires covering concepts thoroughly so the model can pull from your content no matter how the question is phrased. [overthetopseo](https://www.overthetopseo.com/geo-vs-traditional-seo/)
- **From pages to passages**: AI systems often retrieve and synthesize specific sentences or tables, not just rank documents. That pushes you to make key facts “snippable” and well‑structured. [contently](https://contently.com/2026/02/11/geo-vs-traditional-seo/)
- **From static to dynamic visibility**: The same question can yield different AI answers and citations over time, making measurement and monitoring more complex than tracking static SERP positions. [overthetopseo](https://www.overthetopseo.com/geo-vs-traditional-seo/)

## What hasn’t changed (the foundation)

Many SEO basics still matter for GEO:

- High‑quality, authoritative content  
- Strong site structure and crawlability  
- Trust signals (backlinks, brand authority, reviews)  
- Clear headings, internal linking, and good UX  

GEO adds emphasis on **extractability and attributability**: clear definitions, consistent entities, structured data/schema, stats with sources, and content formats AI can easily parse (FAQs, lists, tables). [thriveagency](https://thriveagency.com/news/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo/)

## Practical shift in tactics

If you’re used to traditional SEO, GEO asks you to:

- Write **concept‑rich, comprehensive pages** that answer full questions, not just target a keyword. [overthetopseo](https://www.overthetopseo.com/geo-vs-traditional-seo/)
- Make **key facts explicit**: numbers, definitions, comparisons, and “why this matters” statements. [contently](https://contently.com/2026/02/11/geo-vs-traditional-seo/)
- Use **structured data** (schema) and clean HTML so AI can reliably extract and attribute your content. [thriveagency](https://thriveagency.com/news/generative-engine-optimization-vs-traditional-seo/)
- Track **AI visibility**: how often your brand/content appears in AI answers, not just rankings and clicks. [contently](https://contently.com/2026/02/11/geo-vs-traditional-seo/)

What is LLM readability?

**LLM readability** is a measure of how easily and accurately large language models (LLMs) can process, segment, and extract meaning from a piece of text—especially when that text is retrieved in small “chunks” to answer a query.  It is distinct from traditional human readability (which focuses on how easy text is for people to read) and instead emphasizes machine-oriented qualities like structural clarity, semantic unambiguity, and chunk independence. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/ultimate-guide-for-llm-readability-optimization-and-better-chunk-relevance)

## Core idea

- **Human readability** asks: “Can a person understand this quickly?” (often measured by grade-level formulas like Flesch–Kincaid). [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11150v3)
- **LLM readability** asks: “Can an AI system reliably parse, embed, summarize, and cite this content when it appears as an isolated passage?” [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)

In retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and AI Overviews, systems typically retrieve short passages rather than whole pages. If each passage is self-contained, well-structured, and semantically clear, the model can form a coherent embedding and use the content confidently—increasing the chance it gets cited as a source. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)

## Why it matters

High LLM readability improves:

- **Retrieval quality**: Clear, self-contained chunks produce better vector embeddings and match queries more precisely. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)
- **Summarization accuracy**: Models can compress and restate facts with less risk of hallucination when the source is unambiguous. [media-village.co](https://www.media-village.co.uk/marketing/llm-readable-content-guide/)
- **Citation likelihood**: Content that’s easy to extract and verify is more often quoted in AI-generated answers (e.g., AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity). [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/ultimate-guide-for-llm-readability-optimization-and-better-chunk-relevance)

## Key components of LLM-readable content

Research and practitioner guides converge on several practical factors: [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/ultimate-guide-for-llm-readability-optimization-and-better-chunk-relevance)

- **Structural predictability**: Clear headings (H2/H3), short self-contained sections, lists, and step-by-step formatting.  
- **Semantic clarity**: Explicit definitions, stable terminology, minimal ambiguity or pronoun dependence on distant context.  
- **Factual density**: High ratio of concrete facts to filler; avoid vague or purely stylistic prose.  
- **Chunk independence**: Each section should make sense on its own, not rely on prior paragraphs to be understood.  
- **Entity consistency**: Uniform names for people, products, organizations, and concepts across the document.  
- **Machine-friendly formatting**: Bullets, numbered steps, schema markup (e.g., FAQ, Article), and concise paragraphs (often under ~4 lines).  

## How it’s evaluated

Unlike human readability formulas (e.g., Flesch Reading Ease), LLM readability is often assessed indirectly via:

- **Embedding coherence**: Whether a passage yields a stable, meaningful vector representation. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)
- **Retrieval performance**: How often the passage is selected for relevant queries in RAG pipelines. [media-village.co](https://www.media-village.co.uk/marketing/llm-readable-content-guide/)
- **Citation frequency**: How often AI systems quote or reference the content in generated answers. [kopp-online-marketing](https://www.kopp-online-marketing.com/ultimate-guide-for-llm-readability-optimization-and-better-chunk-relevance)

Some practitioners also test by prompting LLMs to summarize or answer questions using only isolated passages and measuring accuracy and faithfulness. [media-village.co](https://www.media-village.co.uk/marketing/llm-readable-content-guide/)

## Example

A human-readable paragraph might read smoothly but depend on earlier context:  
> “As mentioned above, the protocol uses three phases to ensure consistency.”  

An LLM-readable version would be self-contained:  
> “The consensus protocol uses three phases: (1) proposal, (2) validation, and (3) commitment. Each phase ensures consistency across distributed nodes.”  

The second version can be embedded and cited accurately even when retrieved alone. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/llm-readability/)

In short, LLM readability optimizes content for how AI systems actually consume text—not for how humans read it linearly—making it a cornerstone of **LLM Optimization (LLMO)** and **Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)** strategies. [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/llm-readability-chunk-relevance-most-influential-factors-kopp--1atde)

What is Retrieval Probability in AI search?

**Retrieval Probability** in AI search is the estimated likelihood that a particular document, webpage, passage, or content chunk will be selected during the search system’s retrieval stage for a user’s query.

In traditional information retrieval, the closely related idea is the probability that document \(d\) is relevant to query \(q\), written as:

\[
P(\text{relevant} \mid d,q)
\]

Systems use this kind of estimate to rank potentially useful documents. [cs.cornell](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs630/2006sp/guides/lec4.bl.pdf)

## In AI search

For systems using semantic search or retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), Retrieval Probability usually means something more practical:

> Given a query, how likely is this content to enter the candidate set that the AI will use to construct its answer?

For example, if someone asks, “What are the benefits of heat pumps?”, an AI search system may:

1. Convert the query into an embedding.
2. Search an index of webpages or passages.
3. Retrieve content with similar meaning.
4. Rank or filter the retrieved content.
5. Use selected sources to generate an answer and possibly citations.

A page can therefore be accurate and authoritative but still have low Retrieval Probability if its wording, structure, or topic signals do not match the query well.

## What it does not mean

Retrieval Probability is **not necessarily a published, directly observable percentage**. In many AI-search and generative-engine-optimization discussions, it is an estimated or conceptual metric rather than a standard score exposed by search engines. One recent GEO usage defines it as the likelihood that a specific content chunk is selected during vector retrieval. [thegeolab](https://thegeolab.net/retrieval-probability/)

It is also different from:

| Metric | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Retrieval Probability | Likelihood that content is retrieved as a candidate |
| Ranking probability | Likelihood that it appears near the top of the retrieved results |
| Citation probability | Likelihood that the AI actually cites it |
| Answer visibility | Whether the brand or content appears in the final generated response |

Retrieval is an early gate: if content is not retrieved, it generally cannot be cited in that answer.

## What can increase it?

Typical factors include:

- Clear, explicit answers to specific questions.
- Terminology that matches how users phrase queries.
- Well-defined topical sections rather than vague or purely promotional copy.
- Strong semantic coverage of the subject and related concepts.
- Crawlable, indexable pages with consistent metadata.
- Reliable facts and references that reinforce the page’s topic.
- Content divided into meaningful passages that can stand alone.

For instance, a section titled “How long do heat-pump systems last?” with a direct answer is more easily matched to that question than a page that only says “Our advanced heating solutions are built to perform.”

In short, **Retrieval Probability describes discoverability during the AI’s source-selection process—not the overall quality of the content or whether the AI ultimately uses it in its answer.**

What is GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation)?

**Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)** is the practice of structuring digital content and managing your online presence so that AI-powered answer engines (like Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini) cite, quote, or recommend your brand when generating responses to user queries. [searchengineland](https://searchengineland.com/what-is-generative-engine-optimization-geo-444418)

## How GEO differs from traditional SEO

| Aspect | Traditional SEO | Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) |
|--------|----------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Goal** | Rank highly in a list of blue links on SERPs. | Be selected as a source within AI-generated answers.  [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html) |
| **Success metric** | Organic clicks, impressions, rankings. | Mentions, citations, and inclusion in AI summaries.  [searchengineland](https://searchengineland.com/what-is-generative-engine-optimization-geo-444418) |
| **Optimisation focus** | Keywords, backlinks, page speed, meta tags. | Clarity, factual accuracy, structured data, entity relationships, and brand narrative across the web.  [coursera](https://www.coursera.org/articles/what-is-generative-engine-optimization) |
| **User behaviour** | Users scan results and click through. | Users often get a direct answer without clicking any links.  [conductor](https://www.conductor.com/academy/generative-engine-optimization/) |

## Core pillars of GEO

Most frameworks describe GEO as resting on four interlocking pillars: [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)

1. **Technical foundation**  
   Ensure AI crawlers (e.g., GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Googlebot) can access your site and that you use clear inclusion rules via `robots.txt`, sitemaps, and emerging standards like `llm.txt`. [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)

2. **Structured data & entities**  
   Use schema markup (JSON‑LD) to explicitly define your brand, products, people, and relationships so LLMs can parse them as discrete entities rather than just text. [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)

3. **Content design for AI**  
   Write clearly, factually, and with strong structure (headings, bullet lists, FAQs). Emphasise E‑E‑A‑T signals (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) and unique insights that models are likely to treat as authoritative. [blog.hubspot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/generative-engine-optimization)

4. **Brand narrative across the ecosystem**  
   Manage how your brand appears on third‑party sites (reviews, news, Wikipedia, forums, social platforms). AI models learn from this distributed signal, not just your own domain. [deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ch/en/services/consulting/perspectives/the-age-of-geo.html)

## Why GEO matters now

As more searches end with an AI-generated summary rather than a click to a website, visibility increasingly depends on whether your content is *used* by generative engines, not just whether it ranks.  GEO is therefore seen as the evolution of SEO for the AI era: instead of optimising only for links, you optimise for inclusion in the answer itself.
